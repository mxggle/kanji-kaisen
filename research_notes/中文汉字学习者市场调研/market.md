# Mandarin Chinese learner market worldwide (size, growth, demographics, motivations), as of Oct 2026

Scope note: these notes size the audience for a free web atlas of Chinese characters built around radicals and character origins, and compare it with the Japanese-learner market. The Japanese benchmarks (Japan Foundation ~4M classroom learners abroad in 2024; JLPT ~1.94M applicants in 2025; Japanese #4 on Duolingo 2025) come from the research brief. They are used here only for comparison and were not re-verified in this pass, except the Duolingo rank, which is confirmed below.

Source-quality legend used throughout:
- **[GOV-PRC]**: People's Republic of China government body or state media (Ministry of Education, CLEC, China Daily, CGTN). These are promotional figures with undefined methods. Use with caution.
- **[GOV-TW]**: Taiwan government or Taiwan state news agency (CNA).
- **[INDEP]**: independent survey or academic body.
- **[CO]**: company's own report or marketing.
- **[2ND]**: secondary aggregator, blog or search snippet. Not checked against the primary source.

## 1. How many people learn Mandarin? (official claims, HSK/YCT test-takers, independent estimates)

### Takeaway
Beijing's headline claim is "over 30 million" current learners outside China (Vice-Minister of Education, July 2025), and CLEC cited "210 million" learners in May 2026. Neither figure has a published method, and the 210M is almost certainly a cumulative or broader count. The hardest number available is HSK test volume: about 719,000 sittings in 2024 and about 750,000–810,000 in 2025, depending on whether the count is paid HSK only or the whole HSK family. That is well under half the JLPT's ~1.94M applicants.

### Cited Findings
**Official PRC learner claims (all [GOV-PRC], none independently verified)**
- July 2025: Vice-Minister of Education Du Jiangfeng said the number of Chinese learners worldwide "now exceeds 30 million". The Ministry of Education (MoE) says China supports Chinese learning in 190+ countries and regions, and 86 countries have put Chinese into their national education systems. In the same article, Prof. Zhang Xinsheng (Richmond American University London) said about 90% of learners are outside China, and that many study "only two hours a week during a 12-week term." — [China Daily, 28 Jul 2025](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202507/28/WS6886c96aa310ad07b5d92470.html)
- May 2026: CLEC reported 210 million "global Chinese learners", as relayed by CGTN on 20 Oct 2026. The article does not say whether this means current or cumulative learners. — [CGTN, 20 Oct 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-20/Who-s-learning-Mandarin-200-million-learners-celebrity-kids-and-more-1RoQsZO3Hgc/p.html)
- Earlier official series, from search-result summaries that were not fetched in full [GOV-PRC][2ND]: about 25 million learners outside China (2020, vice-minister); about 20 million outside China (2021 MoE statement); 82 countries with Chinese in national education systems (Foreign Ministry, Apr 2023); 85 countries (2024 MoE-centre report). — [China Daily, Jun 2021](https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202106/08/WS60bf4385498e082fcaef7188/chinese-language-learning-going-global.html); [CGTN, 19 Apr 2024](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2024-04-19/How-many-people-are-learning-Chinese-as-a-foreign-language--1sV3bqZZTKo/p.html)
- For context, Ethnologue (2025) counts 1.18 billion Mandarin speakers: 990M native and 194M second-language. These are speakers, not learners. — [CGTN, 20 Oct 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-20/Who-s-learning-Mandarin-200-million-learners-celebrity-kids-and-more-1RoQsZO3Hgc/p.html)

**HSK test volumes ([GOV-PRC]: CLEC / Chinese Testing International)**
- 2004: about 32,000 HSK candidates. 2024: about 719,000, a more than 22-fold rise. H1 2025: 414,000 candidates, +19.6% year on year. Full-year 2025 was projected at about 750,000, with the cumulative total expected to pass 8.5M. There are 1,477 test centres with 30,000+ test rooms in 168 countries. — [China Daily (Study China), 12 Nov 2025](https://studychina.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202511/12/WS6913edf8498e23165e06a5cc/hsk-3-0-to-be-launched-as-tests-global-reach-expands.html)
- 2025 actual: about 810,000 HSK test-takers, with a cumulative total above 8.5M by end-2025 and "21-fold" growth from 2004 to 2024. The article does not say whether 810,000 covers paid HSK only or HSK plus HSKK, YCT and BCT. — [CGTN, 20 Oct 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-20/Who-s-learning-Mandarin-200-million-learners-celebrity-kids-and-more-1RoQsZO3Hgc/p.html)
- **Conflict:** a third-party stats page gives about 810,000 across all HSK-family exams for **2024**, with a cumulative 7.93M. It also warns that learner counts measure people while HSK counts measure test sittings. — [Shadowing Korean HSK stats (2ND)](https://shadowingkorean.com/hsk-stats/). The year it assigns to the 810k disagrees with CGTN. A CLEC post seen only as a search snippet gives 433,000 test-takers in 157 countries in H1 2025 (+19%), alongside the 414,000 "paid" figure. That fits a broad HSK-family count running about 5% above paid HSK. — [CLEC on X (snippet only)](https://x.com/CLECOfficial/status/1945411379449778416)
- HSK 3.0: the new syllabus and tutorials were released at the World Chinese Language Conference in Beijing in mid-Nov 2025. The new exam debuts in the second half of 2026. All levels get shorter exams (Level 4 drops from 100 to 70 questions), and Levels 7–9 now test translation for 7 foreign languages, up from 5 at the 2022 launch. CTI's Huang Lei: "I am confident that the number of test-takers will continue its upward trajectory." — [China Daily, 12 Nov 2025](https://studychina.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202511/12/WS6913edf8498e23165e06a5cc/hsk-3-0-to-be-launched-as-tests-global-reach-expands.html)
- Russia had 29,917 HSK candidates in 2025, sixth in the world, according to a Russian representative quoted by TV BRICS. — [TV BRICS (2ND)](https://tvbrics.com/en/news/expert-russia-ranks-6th-in-world-by-number-of-chinese-language-exam-candidates/)

**Independent estimates**
- I found no independent (non-PRC-government) global estimate of Mandarin learners. The nearest independent data are national: MLA (US higher ed), American Councils (US K-12), DfE/JCQ (UK) and ACARA (Australia). See section 2.

### Inferences
- The 30M "current learners" claim (2025) and the 210M figure (2026) cannot both describe concurrent learners: that would mean 7x growth in about a year. The 210M is best treated as cumulative ("have learned or are learning") or as including in-China/minority-language learners. Use **~30M (PRC claim, 2025)** as the official upper bound for current foreign learners, and flag it as promotional.
- Implied learners per test sitting: Chinese ≈ 30M / ~0.8M ≈ **37 learners per HSK sitting**. Japanese ≈ 4M / 1.94M ≈ **2 classroom learners per JLPT applicant** (brief-supplied figures). The gap suggests either that the Chinese figure counts many low-intensity learners (school pupils taking compulsory or taster Chinese across Southeast Asia, Africa and the Gulf, at "two hours a week" per Zhang), or that it is inflated, or both. Measured by the people who actually test, the committed Chinese-learner base outside China (~0.75–0.81M HSK sittings in 2025, plus ~0.21M TOCFL) is **about half the JLPT's size**.
- HSK growth is real and fast in percentage terms: about 22x from 2004 to 2024, +19.6% in H1 2025, and +4% to +13% for the 2025 full year depending on which 2025 figure is used. A large share of HSK takers are in Asia (Southeast Asia, Korea, Japan) and are career- or scholarship-motivated. Those are not obviously English-language web users.

### Gaps
- No published method for the 30M or 210M figures. No breakdown by country, age, or intensity.
- No official HSK split by country for 2024–2025 was found in a primary source. The Shadowing Korean regional page was not fetched.
- YCT (the children's test) volumes were not found separately.
- It is unclear whether the 2025 figure of 810k counts paid HSK only or all exams. CLEC's own year-end 2025 release was not located.

## 2. Trends since 2019: Confucius Institute closures; US, UK and Australia; growth regions

### Takeaway
In the Anglosphere, Mandarin in formal education has stagnated or declined since about 2013–2016. US college enrolment fell 14.3% between 2016 and 2021 and now trails Japanese, and US Confucius Institutes have almost all gone (96 in 2019, about 5–10 left). The UK is the exception, with growth driven by a government-funded programme. Growth is now in the Global South: Southeast Asia, the Gulf (Saudi Arabia), Russia, Africa, and Latin America on Duolingo.

### Cited Findings
**US higher education: MLA census, fall 2021 (latest published) [INDEP]**
- Chinese/Mandarin enrolments: 61,612 (2009), 61,997 (2013), 54,248 (2016), **46,492 (2021)**. That is −12.5% from 2013 to 2016 and **−14.3% from 2016 to 2021**. Japanese: 72,357 (2009), 66,771 (2013), 68,810 (2016), **65,661 (2021)**, −4.6%. Korean: 13,936 (2016) to 19,270 (2021), **+38.3%**. All languages: 1,418,584 to 1,182,562 (−16.6%). Japanese is now the 4th most-studied language in US higher education, after Spanish, French and ASL. Chinese is 6th, ahead of Italian. — [MLA, Enrollments in Languages Other Than English, Fall 2021 (Table 1a)](https://www.mla.org/content/download/191324/file/Enrollments-in-Languages-Other-Than-English-in-US-Institutions-of-Higher-Education-Fall-2021.pdf)
- Chinese fell 23.9% at two-year schools, 12.5% at four-year schools and 29.5% at graduate level (graduate: 1,266 to 892, the first such fall in about 20 years). 105 fewer institutions reported Chinese. Even so, Chinese was the only language with growth in the number of institutions at some level. — [MLA Fall 2021](https://www.mla.org/content/download/191324/file/Enrollments-in-Languages-Other-Than-English-in-US-Institutions-of-Higher-Education-Fall-2021.pdf); 61.2% of Mandarin programmes were shrinking in 2021, against 48.8% of Japanese programmes — [Pinyin News, 2023](https://pinyin.info/news/2023/us-postsecondary-enrollments-in-chinese-trending-down/)
- Chinese has a strong introductory-to-advanced ratio (3:1, meaning advanced classes make up 20%+ of enrolments). Japanese is at 6:1. This suggests Chinese students who stay go further, possibly reflecting heritage learners. — [MLA Fall 2021](https://www.mla.org/content/download/191324/file/Enrollments-in-Languages-Other-Than-English-in-US-Institutions-of-Higher-Education-Fall-2021.pdf)

**US K-12**
- The latest national K-12 survey (American Councils, published 2017, data from 2014–15) counted **227,086** Chinese learners, the third most-studied language. An earlier ACTFL report showed K-12 public-school Chinese rising from 20,292 (2004–05) to 59,860. — [American Councils FLE report (2017)](https://www.americancouncils.org/sites/default/files/FLE-report.pdf) [INDEP]
- An ACTFL report (2026), as relayed by CGTN, found Chinese taught in **6%** of public high schools that offer foreign languages. — [CGTN, 20 Oct 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-20/Who-s-learning-Mandarin-200-million-learners-celebrity-kids-and-more-1RoQsZO3Hgc/p.html) [GOV-PRC relaying INDEP; primary not checked]
- SCMP reports that US university Chinese enrolment is flat while K-12 Chinese immersion programmes are expanding. The article's date was not confirmed. — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/global-economy/article/3364145/us-k-12-immersion-schools-expand-across-country-early-age-chinese-language-education) [2ND]

**Confucius Institutes (CIs)**
- US: GAO (Oct 2023) found only 5 CIs still operating at US universities, down from 96 in 44 states in 2019. — [GAO-24-105981](https://www.gao.gov/assets/d24105981high.pdf); see also [Bloomberg, 2 Nov 2023](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-11-02/china-s-confucius-institutes-are-disappearing-from-us-campuses)
- NAS counted 118 CIs that ever existed in the US. 104 were closed and 4 were closing as of June 2022. Its current tracker (date unclear) lists about 10 open. — [National Association of Scholars](https://www.nas.org/articles/how_many_confucius_institutes_are_in_the_united_states) [advocacy group]
- Some programmes have reportedly reopened under new names. — [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/controversial-confucius-institutes-returning-to-u-s-schools-under-new-name/6635906.html) [title-level only]
- Oct 2026: the US has imposed funding restrictions on CIs since 2025 and designated them a threat. China condemned the designation. — [The Star / AFP, 26 Oct 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/10/26/china-condemns-us-designation-of-confucius-institutes-as-a-threat)
- Europe: Sweden closed its last CI or classroom by April 2020, the first European country to close all of them. — [National Review](https://www.nationalreview.com/news/sweden-closes-its-last-remaining-confucius-institute-amid-strained-relations-with-china/amp/). Finland followed. — [ScandAsia](https://scandasia.com/finland-latest-scandinavian-country-to-close-confucius-institute/). Japan: Kansai Gaidai closed its CI in Oct 2025, as Japanese universities "quietly reduce" CIs. — [ChinaAid, Mar 2026](https://chinaaid.org/news/japanese-universities-quietly-reduce-confucius-institutes-growing-wary-of-expanding-foreign-influence/) [advocacy group]
- Russia, by contrast, has 19 CIs and 5 Confucius Classrooms. — [TV BRICS](https://tvbrics.com/en/news/expert-russia-ranks-6th-in-world-by-number-of-chinese-language-exam-candidates/) [2ND]

**UK (growth, policy-driven)**
- GCSE Chinese: a little over 3,000 entries in 2012–13, more than 7,800 in 2023–24 (British Council data, via China Daily). In 2019, 33% of GCSE Chinese entries came from independent schools, which educate about 7% of pupils (DfE / ISC). — [China Daily HK, 2 Jan 2026](https://www.chinadailyhk.com/article/626388) [GOV-PRC citing UK data]. GCSE Mandarin reached 7,854 entries in 2023. — [TES](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/secondary/demand-study-mandarin-outstrips-supply)
- In 2025, 31% of state-funded secondary schools in England had at least one GCSE Chinese entrant (parliamentary data, July 2026, via CGTN). — [CGTN, 20 Oct 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-20/Who-s-learning-Mandarin-200-million-learners-celebrity-kids-and-more-1RoQsZO3Hgc/p.html) [GOV-PRC relay; primary not checked; looks high]
- Mandarin Excellence Programme: launched 2016 with 14 schools and under 400 pupils, against a target of 5,000. It passed **16,000** participants in 2025, with funding extended to Aug 2026 and an expansion into sixth form in 2023. — [China Daily HK, 2 Jan 2026](https://www.chinadailyhk.com/article/626388)
- A-level Chinese: 3,334 entries in 2018, overtaking German. — [Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/a-level-results-2018-chinese-a-level-overtakes-german-for-first-time/). 1,643 in 2024, up from 1,449 in 2023, but still below the 2,272 of 2019. — [Guernsey Press / PA, 15 Aug 2024](https://guernseypress.com/news/uk-news/2024/08/15/rise-in-entries-for-a-levels-in-russian-and-chinese). The 2018 and 2024 figures may use different scopes (UK-wide vs England, or including native speakers), which would explain the gap.

**Australia (decline; heritage-dominated)**
- About 4,000 Year 12 students study Chinese, and an estimated ~400 of them are of non-Chinese heritage. Non-background classroom learners fell about 20% between 2007 and 2014. — [The Conversation, 2019](https://theconversation.com/china-crisis-hardly-it-doesnt-matter-most-aussie-kids-dont-speak-fluent-mandarin-119647); [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/australia-missing-out-as-students-continue-to-shun-learning-chinese) [figures attributed via search summary; the exact sentence was not confirmed]
- An older federal report found only 4,149 Year 12 Chinese learners: 2.4% of all Chinese learners and 0.1% of the cohort. — [Australian Govt, Current state of Chinese, Indonesian, Japanese and Korean education (c. 2010)](https://education.gov.au/download/1037/current-state-chinese-indonesian-japanese-and-korean-language-education-australian-schools-summary/772/document/pdf)
- In 2023, only 3.3% of Year 12 students studied Chinese, Indonesian or Japanese, down from 5.1% in 2010 (parliamentary report). — [California Insider / Epoch Times](https://californiainsider.com/news/fewer-australian-students-studying-asian-languages-5944586) [2ND]

**Growth regions**
- Saudi Arabia: 174 public middle schools added Mandarin (Saudi MoE data, Apr 2025). The target is 600,000+ middle and high school students by 2030. — [CGTN, 20 Oct 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-20/Who-s-learning-Mandarin-200-million-learners-celebrity-kids-and-more-1RoQsZO3Hgc/p.html)
- Russia: about 110,000 people are learning Chinese (CGTN, May 2026), and 360+ schools include Chinese in the curriculum. Chinese is an option in the national Unified State Exam (EGE), introduced around 2019; sources disagree on the year. — [CGTN, 18 May 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-05-18/From-classrooms-to-connections-China-Russia-educational-ties-deepen-1NfmIO48vjG/p.html); [TV BRICS](https://tvbrics.com/en/news/expert-russia-ranks-6th-in-world-by-number-of-chinese-language-exam-candidates/)
- Southeast Asia: Chinese is Thailand's second most popular foreign language, according to the Bangkok Post (Jul 2025). — [Bangkok Post via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/hi/stories/newspaper/Bangkok-Post/STRONG-LANGUAGE). Chinese centres are spreading across Indonesia, including Aceh and Papua (Xinhua, May 2026). — [The Star, 4 May 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/05/04/china039s-mandarin-wave-sweeps-indonesia039s-islands). Demand is linked to Chinese manufacturing relocating into the region. — [FN News, Aug 2026](https://en.fnnews.com/news/202608301756264056). A 2019 China Daily article quotes a scholar saying "the majority of overseas Chinese-language learners are from Southeast Asia". — [China Daily, Sep 2019](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/global/2019-09/05/content_37507976.htm) [GOV-PRC]
- Africa: several countries have put Chinese into national curricula, delivered mainly through CIs and Chinese-run schools, and teacher shortages are a bottleneck. — [The Star / Xinhua, Apr 2024 (UNECA official)](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2024/04/20/interest-in-chinese-language-booms-amid-growing-opportunities-uneca-official); [The Star, Apr 2025 (South Africa)](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2025/04/18/roundup-learning-chinese-language-opens-a-lot-of-doors-says-south-african-student) [Xinhua-sourced]
- Latin America and Europe: on Duolingo, Chinese was the **fastest-growing language in 12 countries in 2025**: Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, France, Germany, Indonesia, Mexico, Poland, South Korea, Spain, Thailand and Türkiye. It was also the second-fastest-growing in the US. — [Duolingo 2025 Language Report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2025-duolingo-language-report/) [CO]

### Inferences
- Classroom Chinese in English-speaking countries peaked around 2013–2016 and is now below Japanese in US higher education: Japanese has 1.41x as many enrolments (65,661 vs 46,492 in 2021). Chinese fell about 25% from 2013 to 2021, against about 9% for Japanese from 2009 to 2021. Geopolitics, the CI closures and the post-2020 drop in China study-abroad are plausible drivers. The MLA itself does not attribute causes.
- The UK MEP shows that policy funding can still grow Mandarin. But UK Mandarin skews toward independent schools and stays small (about 8k GCSE entries, against about 16k MEP participants in total).
- In absolute terms, the fastest-growing learner populations (Southeast Asia, Gulf, Russia, Africa) are mostly classroom- and career-driven and not English-first. For an English-language web atlas, the high-growth reachable segment is the Duolingo-style self-learner in Europe, Latin America, Türkiye, Korea, Indonesia, Thailand and the US. Localisation into Spanish, Portuguese, Indonesian or Thai would widen reach.

### Gaps
- The MLA fall 2025 census results were not found (likely unpublished as of Oct 2026). The newest US college data is fall 2021.
- There has been no national US K-12 enrolment survey since the 2017 one (2014–15 data). The 2026 ACTFL report was not checked at the primary source.
- No official JCQ 2025 GCSE or A-level Chinese entry figures were confirmed. No British Council Language Trends 2025 primary data was fetched.
- No current global CI count was found. A snippet claiming "61 institutes and 48 classrooms on the continent in late 2021" was too ambiguous to attribute (it may refer to Africa rather than Europe), so it is excluded.
- No hard learner counts were found for Thailand, Indonesia, Africa or Latin America.

## 3. Duolingo Language Report: rank and growth for Chinese (2023–2025)

### Takeaway
Chinese has been stable in the global top 10: #9 in 2023, #8 in 2024, #8 in 2025. Its momentum is rising: it was the fastest-growing language in 12 countries in 2025 and second-most-popular in 9 countries, up from 5. Japanese sits four places higher, at #4 in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 global top 10: English, Spanish, French, **Japanese (#4, passing German)**, German, Korean (#6, passing Italian), Italian, **Chinese (#8)**, Portuguese, Hindi. Chinese is the fastest-growing language in 12 countries and the second-fastest in the US. It is not #1 in any country and is #2 in 9 countries. Japanese is #2 in 13 countries. — [Duolingo 2025 Language Report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2025-duolingo-language-report/) [CO]
- 2024 global top 10: English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese (#5, steady since 2021), Italian, Korean, **Chinese (#8, "moved up from 9th")**, Portuguese, Hindi. Chinese was #2 in 5 countries. — [Duolingo 2024 Language Report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2024-duolingo-language-report/) [CO]
- The 2023 rank of #9 is confirmed by the 2024 report's statement that Chinese moved up from 9th. — [Duolingo 2024 Language Report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2024-duolingo-language-report/)
- Duolingo motivation data (2024) cover mainly English, Spanish and French. For Japanese, 32% of learners in China study it "just for fun", and Japanese is the #1 language in the Philippines. Duolingo published no motivation breakdown for Chinese learners. Learners under 13 are excluded from analyses. — [Duolingo 2024 Language Report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2024-duolingo-language-report/)
- Only the Chinese-language edition of the 2025 report states "over 500 million learners" in total. — [Duolingo 2025 Language Report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2025-duolingo-language-report/)

### Inferences
- On the main global self-study platform, Japanese draws more learners than Chinese: rank #4 vs #8. Duolingo publishes ranks, not counts, so the ratio cannot be computed. Chinese is gaining fastest in Latin America, continental Europe, Türkiye and parts of Asia, which matches the "fun and culture" self-learner profile an atlas would serve.
- Korean's rise to #6 alongside Japanese shows that pop-culture-driven East Asian language demand is strong. Chinese has not yet turned C-drama, C-pop or gaming into an equivalent pull, though its rising "fastest-growing" status hints it may be starting to.

### Gaps
- Duolingo does not publish Chinese course active-user counts or year-over-year growth percentages.
- No Duolingo data on Chinese learners' motivations, ages, or overlap with Japanese learners.

## 4. Motivations and segments (heritage, career, university requirement, culture/C-drama/gaming, Japanese overlap, age, self-study vs classroom)

### Takeaway
Heritage learners make up a very large share of Western classroom Chinese learners: about half of university samples in small studies, and up to ~90% of Year 12 Chinese students in Australia. Non-heritage motivation is mostly instrumental (career, travel), with cultural interest a clear second. I found no hard data on age distribution, self-study vs classroom share, C-drama or gaming motivation, or overlap with Japanese.

### Cited Findings
- In one university study, heritage learners were 45.2% of the Chinese-learner sample and non-heritage 54.8%. The top four reasons heritage students gave were "Mandarin Chinese is interesting", "interest in Chinese culture", "to get a better job" and "useful when travelling". Heritage students showed significantly stronger intrinsic motivation. — [Wen, Heritage Language Journal (2011)](https://www.uh.edu/class/mcl/faculty/wen_x/_pdf/2011-chinese-language-learning-motivationhlj.pdf); [ERIC ED539385](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED539385.pdf) [INDEP, small samples; which paper holds the 45.2% figure was not confirmed]
- In a New York State study of mixed classrooms, 59 students were heritage and 61 non-heritage. — search summary of [ResearchGate: Heritage Learners in the Chinese Language Classroom](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234648421_Heritage_Learners_in_the_Chinese_Language_Classroom_Home_Background) [2ND]
- Chinese heritage motivation centres on identity, heritage, and the belief that proficiency brings career opportunities. — [PMC: Chinese heritage language motivation (2024)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11554449/). Heritage learners in California high schools differed significantly from others on a "Cultural Heritage" factor. — [ERIC ED657611 (2024)](https://eric.ed.gov/?q=jasmine&ff1=subTeacher+Attitudes&id=ED657611)
- Australia: of about 4,000 Year 12 Chinese students, an estimated ~400 are non-Chinese heritage. — [The Conversation, 2019](https://theconversation.com/china-crisis-hardly-it-doesnt-matter-most-aussie-kids-dont-speak-fluent-mandarin-119647)
- Career and economy: Southeast Asian demand is tied to Chinese manufacturers moving into the region. — [FN News, Aug 2026](https://en.fnnews.com/news/202608301756264056). A South African student said Chinese "opens a lot of doors". — [The Star, Apr 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2025/04/18/roundup-learning-chinese-language-opens-a-lot-of-doors-says-south-african-student)
- University requirement and scholarships: HSK is required for Chinese university admission and scholarships, and the Indonesian and Japanese students profiled took HSK 5 for study and career. — [China Daily, Nov 2025](https://studychina.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202511/12/WS6913edf8498e23165e06a5cc/hsk-3-0-to-be-launched-as-tests-global-reach-expands.html)
- Low-intensity classroom learning is common: "only two hours a week during a 12-week term". — [China Daily, Jul 2025](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202507/28/WS6886c96aa310ad07b5d92470.html)
- Celebrity and elite signalling (children of celebrities learning Mandarin) is a theme in PRC state media. — [CGTN, Oct 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-20/Who-s-learning-Mandarin-200-million-learners-celebrity-kids-and-more-1RoQsZO3Hgc/p.html) [GOV-PRC, anecdotal]
- For comparison, MLA's Kansas case study found that most Korean students enrol because of interest in Korean popular culture. The MLA attributes Korean's +38.3% growth partly to pop culture, a driver it does not attribute to Chinese. — [MLA Fall 2021](https://www.mla.org/content/download/191324/file/Enrollments-in-Languages-Other-Than-English-in-US-Institutions-of-Higher-Education-Fall-2021.pdf)

### Inferences
- Segments relevant to a radicals and origins atlas, in rough order of fit:
  1. Heritage learners (overseas Chinese families). They have speaking or listening ability but weak literacy, so characters are their main gap. They are a large share of Western classroom learners.
  2. Self-directed hobby learners from Duolingo-type channels. This is the growth segment.
  3. University and HSK students who need memorisation aids.
  4. Japanese learners who want kanji etymology. Origin material (oracle bone to seal script) is shared, so one atlas could serve both markets.
  5. Calligraphy and script-history enthusiasts.
- Heritage learners skew toward Traditional characters in some diasporas (Hong Kong, Taiwan, older Cantonese communities) and Simplified in others (mainland emigrants). That favours offering both scripts.
- Since no data exist on cross-study with Japanese, the overlap claim must remain anecdotal.

### Gaps
- No reliable global or national age distribution of Chinese learners was found.
- No self-study vs classroom split was found for Chinese (Japan Foundation data covers only classroom learners for Japanese).
- No survey quantifying C-drama, C-pop or gaming (e.g., Genshin Impact, Black Myth: Wukong) as motivations was found.
- No data on the share of Chinese learners who also study Japanese.
- No global count of heritage learners (overseas Chinese weekend schools, etc.) was located in this pass.

## 5. Simplified vs Traditional, and Taiwan's Mandarin programmes (TOCFL)

### Takeaway
No source measures what share of learners study each script. The best proxy is test volume. TOCFL (Taiwan) sittings doubled from about 104k (2023) to about 214k (2025) and reached about 206k in the first eight months of 2026, against about 750k–810k HSK sittings in 2025. On that basis, roughly one in five standardised Chinese proficiency tests is Taiwan's. Treat the proxy with caution, because TOCFL includes test-takers in Taiwan and its recent surge has not been explained.

### Cited Findings
- TOCFL sittings per year: 110 (2003, first administration); 10,000+ (2008); 42,468 (2013); 68,710 (2019); 82,334 (2022); **103,549 (2023)**; **154,676 (2024)**; **213,882 (2025)**; **206,294 (Jan–Aug 2026)**. Cumulative: 1,313,956 (2003 to Aug 2026). Overseas testing runs in 77 countries: 22 in the Americas, 26 in Europe, 22 in Asia, 3 in Africa and 4 in Oceania. — [CNA, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.cna.com.tw/postwrite/chi/446481) [GOV-TW]
- The 2023 figure was a record at over 100,000. — [Language Magazine, 26 Mar 2024](https://languagemagazine.com/2024/03/26/taiwan-chinese-test-breaks-record)
- Milestones: overseas testing began in 2006 (US, UK, Japan, Korea, Thailand). Computerised testing arrived in 2011 and adaptive testing in 2016. The 4-band, 8-level structure dates from 2017. Home-based testing began in 2021. — [CNA, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.cna.com.tw/postwrite/chi/446481)
- An older Taiwan MoE figure, relayed by Taiwan Today, put the cumulative total at about 300,000 with about 40,000 a year. That is consistent with the pre-2015 series above. — search summary [2ND]

### Inferences
- TOCFL's share of combined HSK and TOCFL sittings in 2025 is 213,882 / (213,882 + 810,000) ≈ **21%**, or ≈ **22%** using the 750k paid-HSK projection. At the 2026 pace (206k by August), TOCFL could reach about 300k in 2026 if the rate holds. That would be a third straight year of 38–50% growth, against high single-digit to mid-teens growth for HSK. This is an extrapolation, not a reported figure.
- The proxy almost certainly *overstates* the share of learners using Traditional characters. TOCFL volume includes people tested inside Taiwan (international students, possibly migrant workers and spouses), and the 2024–25 jump may come from new policy or test-type changes not explained in the source. Still, Traditional characters are a non-trivial minority (low tens of percent) of formal test-takers, so an atlas showing both scripts plus the historical forms has a clear differentiation case. Traditional forms are also closer to seal and bronze forms, which suits the origins story.
- Hong Kong, Macau, and many older diaspora heritage schools use Traditional, and kanji learners meet forms closer to Traditional (e.g., 學→学 vs 学). This adds Traditional-relevant users beyond the TOCFL count.

### Gaps
- No survey or official data on the share of global learners studying Simplified vs Traditional.
- No breakdown of TOCFL takers by domestic vs overseas, or by type (international students, migrant workers, overseas centres).
- No 2024–2026 enrolment figures were found for Taiwan's university Mandarin centres, the Taiwan Centers for Mandarin Learning (TCML) network, or the Huayu Enrichment Scholarship.
- Whether TOCFL offers a Simplified-character version (which would complicate the proxy) was not confirmed in this pass.

## 6. Willingness to pay and price points of popular Chinese-learning tools

### Takeaway
Serious Chinese learners pay for tools: one-time dictionary bundles at $25–$60, subscriptions at about $70–$120 a year. Character etymology is a paid niche already. Outlier's Dictionary of Chinese Characters sells inside Pleco for $29.99 or $59.99. A free, well-designed origins atlas would undercut a proven paid product.

### Cited Findings
- **Pleco** (the standard dictionary; free core app with paid add-ons): Basic Bundle **$24.99**, Professional Bundle **$49.99**. Individual add-ons: OCR $9.99, Document Reader $9.99, Flashcards $9.99, Stroke Order $3.99. **Outlier Dictionary of Chinese Characters** (character explanations and etymology): Essentials **$29.99**, Expert **$59.99**. Graded readers cost $2.99–$9.99 each, and bundles $24.99–$159.99. — [Pleco Store](https://store.pleco.com/products.php) [CO]. **Conflict:** a search snippet of Pleco's iOS pricing page gives the Professional Bundle as $59.99, and says Outlier Essentials covers etymology for about 3,000 characters and Expert adds expert explanations for 250. — [Pleco iOS pricing page (snippet)](https://www.pleco.com/products/pleco-for-iphone-ipad/pricing-bundles). The prices may differ by platform or date.
- **Skritter** (character-writing SRS; one subscription covers Chinese and Japanese): **$14.99/month, $59.99 per 6 months, $99.99/year**, with a 7-day trial, per a 2026 review. The App Store lists Premium 1-year at $119.99 and 6-month at $69.99. — [Discover Discomfort, Skritter review (2026)](https://discoverdiscomfort.com/skritter-review-learn-chinese-characters/); [App Store: Skritter Write Chinese](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1370892114). Older prices: $9.95/mo (2012), $15.99/mo (2023 review). — [Skritter blog, 2012](https://blog.skritter.com/2012/11/skritter-subscriptions/)
- **HelloChinese** (freemium course): Premium at **$11.99/month, $25.99 per 3 months, $69.99/year** (US). — [Similarweb app listing](https://similarweb.com/zh/app/apple/1001507516) [2ND]
- Pricing models (2026): Du Chinese is freemium (a few free stories a week). Skritter is paid subscription only. Superchinese, LingoDeer, Chineasy (a picture-based character approach) and Duolingo are freemium. The Chairman's Bao and ChineseClass101 are paid subscriptions. Rocket Chinese is a one-time lifetime purchase. italki and Preply charge per lesson. — [PREP, Best Apps to Learn Chinese 2026 (updated 29 Oct 2026)](https://prepedu.com/en/blog/chinese-learning-apps) [2ND]
- Du Chinese: a French retailer page suggests about €99.99 for 12 months, but its attribution to Du Chinese is unclear. — [Groupe Réussite](https://groupe-reussite.fr/ressources/cp-chinois-applications-mobiles/) [2ND, unreliable]

### Inferences
- The price range for Chinese tools is roughly $25–$60 one-time (Pleco and add-ons, including the Outlier etymology dictionary) and $70–$120 a year (HelloChinese, Skritter, Du Chinese). These are similar to Japanese tools; Skritter even shares one subscription across Chinese and Japanese.
- Outlier's existence and pricing show that serious learners will pay $30–$60 for character-origin explanations. A free web atlas would compete on access and design, not price. The monetisable demand (if ever wanted) is premium depth (Expert-level analysis), offline or mobile use, and SRS integration.
- Because Pleco is near-universal among serious learners, linking out to Pleco or exporting to Anki or Skritter decks is likely more valuable than competing with dictionary features.

### Gaps
- Du Chinese, Superchinese, Mandarin Blueprint, Chineasy and Hack Chinese prices were not verified at the primary source.
- No data on what share of Chinese learners pay for any tool (conversion rates), or on total Chinese-learning app revenue.
- No Chinese-specific willingness-to-pay survey was found.

## 7. Comparison with the Japanese-learner market (for audience sizing)

### Takeaway
Official claims make Chinese look 7–8x larger than Japanese (30M vs ~4M), but every behavioural or independent measure points the other way, or puts the two near parity. JLPT draws about 2.4–2.6x as many applicants as HSK draws test-takers. Japanese ranks higher on Duolingo (#4 vs #8). US college Japanese enrolments are 1.41x Chinese. For an English-language web atlas, the Chinese audience is likely the same order of magnitude as, or somewhat smaller than, the Japanese one. It is growing faster in non-Anglophone regions, and it carries a larger heritage segment.

### Cited Findings
- HSK test-takers: about 719k (2024) and about 750k–810k (2025). — [China Daily, Nov 2025](https://studychina.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202511/12/WS6913edf8498e23165e06a5cc/hsk-3-0-to-be-launched-as-tests-global-reach-expands.html); [CGTN, Oct 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-20/Who-s-learning-Mandarin-200-million-learners-celebrity-kids-and-more-1RoQsZO3Hgc/p.html)
- TOCFL sittings: 213,882 (2025). — [CNA, Oct 2026](https://www.cna.com.tw/postwrite/chi/446481)
- Duolingo 2025: Japanese #4, Chinese #8. Japanese is #2 in 13 countries and Chinese #2 in 9. Chinese is fastest-growing in 12 countries. — [Duolingo 2025](https://blog.duolingo.com/2025-duolingo-language-report/)
- US higher education, fall 2021: Japanese 65,661 (−4.6% since 2016), Chinese 46,492 (−14.3%). — [MLA Fall 2021](https://www.mla.org/content/download/191324/file/Enrollments-in-Languages-Other-Than-English-in-US-Institutions-of-Higher-Education-Fall-2021.pdf)
- Official "learners": Chinese 30M+ (PRC MoE, 2025). — [China Daily, Jul 2025](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202507/28/WS6886c96aa310ad07b5d92470.html)

### Inferences
- Ratios, using the brief's Japanese figures of JLPT ~1.94M (2025) and Japan Foundation ~4M (2024):
  - JLPT / (HSK + TOCFL) 2025 ≈ 1.94M / (0.81M + 0.21M) ≈ **1.9x**. Against HSK alone, it is ≈ 2.4–2.6x.
  - Official learners, Chinese / Japanese ≈ 30M / 4M ≈ 7.5x, but the definitions differ: undefined PRC counting versus a survey of institutional classroom learners.
  - US college enrolment, Japanese / Chinese ≈ 1.41x (2021).
- A reasonable planning assumption: the online and self-study Chinese-character audience reachable in English is about 0.5–1x the Japanese-kanji audience. On top of that, Chinese adds (a) a heritage-literacy segment that Japanese largely lacks, (b) faster Duolingo growth in Latin America, Europe and Southeast Asia, and (c) a Traditional-script minority. Because both scripts share oracle-bone, bronze and seal origins, an atlas built for one can serve the other at low marginal cost. That likely makes the combined CJK-character-origins audience the strongest positioning.

### Gaps
- The Japanese benchmark figures (Japan Foundation 2024, JLPT 2025) were taken from the brief and not re-verified here.
- No like-for-like measure (e.g., monthly active self-learners by language across apps) exists publicly for Chinese vs Japanese.
- There is no search-volume or web-traffic comparison (e.g., Google Trends for "radical" or "hanzi etymology" vs "kanji etymology"). A Similarweb or Ahrefs pass would be needed and was not done.
