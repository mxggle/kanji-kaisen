# Mandarin Chinese teachers as an audience for a free web character tool (vs. Japanese teachers abroad)

Scope: K-12, university and heritage/weekend-school teachers of Mandarin outside Greater China; needs around teaching characters; tools in use; constraints. Comparison baseline: Japanese teachers abroad (Japan Foundation 2024). Research date: 2026-10-31. Geography is stated for every figure.

## 1. How many Mandarin teachers, programs and students are there abroad, and what are the trends?

### Takeaway
No official worldwide count of Mandarin teachers abroad turned up; China publishes learner totals (about 30M learners abroad) but not teacher totals. The countable formal-school segments are mid-sized and mostly flat or shrinking: about 227k US K-12 students (2017), 18.3k AP Chinese candidates (2025, still growing), US college Chinese down 14.3% (2016 to 2021), about 7.8k GCSE Mandarin entries in England (2023, growing), and declining Australian second-language Chinese. Policy shocks since 2019 include the near-total closure of US Confucius Institutes and the decommissioning of NSA's STARTALK in 2025.

### Cited Findings

**Global (China-reported, learners only)**
- More than 30 million people outside China were learning Chinese, and 85 countries had put Chinese into their national education systems (People's Daily Overseas Edition, Dec 2023, as summarized) — [rmzxw.com.cn](https://www.rmzxw.com.cn/c/2023-12-20/3463132.shtml); [huxiu](https://m.huxiu.com/article/3579973.html)
- China's Ministry of Education put foreign learners at more than 25 million at end-2021. A China Daily 2021 report said "more than 70 countries" had Chinese in their national systems, which conflicts with the 85 figure (different dates and possibly different definitions) — [jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/9321559.html); [China Daily 2021](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202106/03/WS60b8431ca31024ad0bac35fa.html)
- The Chinese International Education Foundation put *registered* learners at more than 12.5 million by 2023, a much narrower definition than the 25-30M headline — [China Daily 2024](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202409/30/WS66f9f31ea310f1265a1c5a0a.html)
- About 180,000 people took YCT/HSK/HSKK/BCT exams worldwide in H1 2024, up about 50% year on year — [jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/11832024.html)
- A 2024 conference paper (ICSSED) describes a shortage of *local* Chinese teachers abroad and weak certification systems in lower-income countries, but gives no headcount — [Atlantis Press](https://atlantis-press.com/article/126001736.pdf)

**United States, K-12**
- The 2017 National K-12 Foreign Language Enrollment Survey (ACTFL/American Councils) counted 227,086 K-12 students in Chinese. That made Chinese the 4th most-taught language, after Spanish (7.36M), French (1.29M) and German (about 331k). Chinese was offered in every state except South Dakota, and one analysis counted 1,144 schools/districts teaching it — [Language Magazine](https://www.languagemagazine.com/2017/06/chinese-highlight-dismal-k-12-enrollment-survey/); [AsAmNews](https://asamnews.com/2017/06/03/interest-in-chinese-korean-and-hindi-growing/)
- Mandarin immersion (US): 190 programs in 27 states + DC (2015), at least 246 programs in 31 states + DC (2017, CELIN), 264 schools (2018), and a projected 306 schools in 2019-20 including 15 new ones (Mandarin Immersion Parents Council). I found no newer count — [MIPC 2015](https://miparentscouncil.org/2015/02/28/were-up-to-190-mandarin-immersion-programs-in-the-us/); [search summary of MIPC/CELIN posts](https://miparentscouncil.org/2014/08/17/updated-map-of-all-mandarin-immersion-programs-in-the-u-s)
- Program cuts (US, anecdotal): Illinois District 214 ended Mandarin at two high schools in 2018 because of falling enrollment. McLean High (VA) dropped Chinese because classes were too small. Blue Valley (KS) closed one of two elementary immersion programs, largely because it could not find licensed Chinese-speaking teachers — [MIPC](https://miparentscouncil.org/2018/05/25/illinois-district-cuts-mandarin-but-immersion-programs-thrive-nearby/); [Highlander News](https://thehighlandernews.com/41919/news/mclean-says-goodbye-to-chinese/); [Foreign Policy 2023](https://foreignpolicy.com/2023/06/16/mandarin-language-school-immersion-geopolitics/)
- SCMP (2024-25) reports that school staff see US Mandarin as plateaued or declining, and that MLA has released no data since 2021. Immersion programs are described as relatively insulated from geopolitics — [SCMP 2024](https://www.scmp.com/economy/global-economy/article/3273862/mandarins-popularity-sags-hits-plateau-us-enthusiasm-wanes-american-youth); [SCMP 2025](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3318841/huge-shift-why-learning-mandarin-losing-its-appeal-west); [SCMP 2023](https://amp.scmp.com/news/china/article/3246088/mandarin-immersion-programmes-persist-american-schools-despite-high-level-us-china-tensions)

**AP Chinese (College Board; mostly US, includes some international test centers)**
- 2025: 18,312 test takers per the College Board score-distribution page, mean score 4.11, 89.2% scoring 3+. The College Board's 2025 PDF shows 18,518, a discrepancy between two College Board documents — [AP Students](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/about-ap-scores/score-distributions/ap-chinese-language-and-culture); [AP Central PDF 2025](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-chinese-language-score-distributions.pdf)

A third-party site claims about 21,000 for 2026, which is unverified — [Wikiwand](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Advanced_Placement_Chinese_Language_and_Culture); [dailyzhongwen](https://www.dailyzhongwen.com/2026/06/student-score-distributions-global-ap.html)

**United States, higher education (MLA Fall 2021 census)**
- Chinese/Mandarin enrollments fell 14.3% from 2016 to 2021: -23.9% at two-year schools (declining since 2009), -12.5% at four-year schools (declining since 2013), and -29.5% at graduate level (1,266 to 892). 105 fewer institutions reported Chinese, and 61.2% of Mandarin programs were shrinking, compared with 48.8% of Japanese programs. Japanese overtook German in total enrollments — [pinyin.info analysis of MLA 2021](https://pinyin.info/news/2023/us-postsecondary-enrollments-in-chinese-trending-down/)
- Across all languages, enrollments fell 16.6% from 2016 to 2021, the largest drop in the census's history. The census surveyed 2,679 institutions — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/us-sees-record-drop-enrolment-languages-courses); [JNCL-NCLIS](https://languagepolicy.org/post/jncl-nclis-statement-on-the-2021-mla-report-on-enrollment-in-higher-education-language-courses)

**United Kingdom (England unless noted)**
- GCSE Mandarin entries more than doubled, from 3,042 (2012) to 7,854 (2023). Independent (private) schools supply about a third of entries while educating 7% of pupils — [Tes](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/secondary/demand-study-mandarin-outstrips-supply)
- China Daily (Nov 2025) gives "more than 7,800" GCSE Mandarin entries in 2023-24, up from just over 3,000 in 2012-13 — [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202511/29/WS6929d49da310d6866eb2bffe.html)
- A-level Chinese (UK): 3,334 entries in 2018 (+8.6%), when it overtook German — [Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/a-level-results-2018-chinese-a-level-overtakes-german-for-first-time/). Tes later reported A-level entries had "more than halved," from 3,425 (2012) to 1,643 (year not shown on page). Tes also notes that native/heritage Chinese candidates skew A-level statistics — [Tes](https://www.tes.com/magazine/analysis/secondary/studying-Mandarin-a-level-is-too-difficult); [Tes](https://www.tes.com/news/native-chinese-pupils-may-be-skewing-stats-level-mandarin)
- Mandarin Excellence Programme (DfE-funded, run by UCL IOE and British Council; England state schools): more than 16,000 pupils as of 2025, more than 5,000 of whom have completed Years 7-11 and sat GCSE. The first MEP GCSE cohort sat in 2021-22 — [UCL IOE MEP](https://ci.ioe.ac.uk/mandarin-excellence-programme); [MEP Evaluation 2023-24](https://ci.ioe.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/MEP-Evaluation-Full-Report-2023-2024.pdf)
- Language Trends England 2025: only 4.3% of responding state schools had Mandarin language assistants, against 36% of the 42 independent schools that responded. More than 16,500 students had taken part in MEP since 2016 — [Tes summarizing Language Trends 2025](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/secondary/demand-study-mandarin-outstrips-supply); [British Council Language Trends 2025](https://www.britishcouncil.org/research-insight/language-trends-england-2025)

**Australia**
- 172,832 school students were learning Chinese (2016 report) — [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/australia-missing-out-as-students-continue-to-shun-learning-chinese)
- Second-language Chinese enrollments fell 23%, while Chinese held 19-21% of all Year 12 language enrollments since 2006. Heritage speakers often enroll in second-language courses, which deters non-background learners — [Crikey](https://stagecdn.crikey.com.au/?p=670399)
- VCE Chinese enrollments (Victoria) fell 23% between 2013 and 2018 — [inkl/Guardian](https://www.inkl.com/news/are-young-australians-being-turned-off-china-as-diplomatic-ties-grow-frostier)
- Only 17 Australian students graduated from Chinese honours programs from 2017 to 2021 — [AIIA](https://www.internationalaffairs.org.au/news-item/building-chinas-expertise-how-to-fix-the-generational-pipeline/)
- Mandarin, Japanese and French are Australia's top three school languages — [California Insider/Epoch](https://californiainsider.com/news/schools-could-adopt-mandarin-immersion-amid-falling-asian-language-enrolments-5956136)

**Confucius Institutes/Classrooms (US)**
- US Confucius Institutes fell from about 100 in 2019 to fewer than 5 (GAO-24-105981). More than 60% of 74 surveyed schools said losing federal (DoD) funding eligibility under the FY2019/FY2021 NDAAs contributed "to a great extent" to closing. After closure, schools replaced support through academic departments (43), US-government language programs (16), Taiwanese entities (12) and the former Chinese partner (9) — [GAO-24-105981](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-105981)
- K-12 Confucius Classrooms: the advocacy group Parents Defending Education (2023) counted 143 districts in 34 states + DC that had received $17.97M since 2009. Rep. Aaron Bean claimed more than 500 K-12 schools host them (not audited). An Ohio Confucius Classroom consortium serving about 1,200 K-12 students kept operating after Miami University closed its Confucius Institute. Most North Carolina Confucius Classrooms had shut by 2020 — [Fox Baltimore](https://www.foxbaltimore.com/news/nation-world/chinese-government-backed-programs-active-in-several-us-public-school-districts-report-finds-confucius-classroom-chinese-communist-party-china-american-intellectual-property-parents-defending-education); [search summary incl. Washington Examiner/Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/national-security/china-backed-confucius-institute-turns-its-attention-to-k-12-classrooms/)
- A Mandarin immersion parents' group says the crackdown on Chinese-government-funded programs has made Mandarin teachers harder to recruit — [MIPC (via search summary)](https://miparentscouncil.org/tag/news/page/2/)

**STARTALK (US federal, NSA-funded summer programs and teacher training)**
- In 2023 STARTALK narrowed funding eligibility to Chinese and Russian only. NSA announced on April 30, 2025 that STARTALK was decommissioned, the portal went offline, and funded programs ran their final cohorts. This rests on Wikipedia; I found no NSA primary source — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STARTALK_(language_program))
- Wofford College (2025): "NSA has canceled funding for next year." Buffalo and Concordia still ran 2025 Chinese STARTALK programs — [Wofford](https://www.wofford.edu/about/news/news-archives/2025/learning-chinese-in-20-days); [UB](https://www.buffalo.edu/ubnow/stories/2025/09/startalk-chinese-program.html)

### Inferences
- Mandarin's formal K-12 and university base in the US and Australia is flat to shrinking, and two subsidy streams (Confucius funding and STARTALK) have been removed since 2019. Formal-school Mandarin teachers are therefore likely more resource-starved than before, which favors a free, no-procurement tool. The same trend also means the segment is not growing.
- The AP Chinese mean of 4.11 and the Tes/Crikey notes on heritage candidates suggest that a large share of high-stakes Chinese exam takers in the US, UK and Australia are heritage learners. Character-tool demand among AP and A-level teachers may lean toward literacy (reading/writing) more than beginner stroke basics.
- England is the one market with documented growth (GCSE entries more than doubled, MEP at 16k+ pupils). MEP schools form a concentrated, reachable cluster through UCL IOE.
- A rough, unverified sizing: 227k US K-12 students at about 100-150 students per teacher implies about 1,500-2,300 US K-12 Chinese teachers. This is my own arithmetic, not a sourced figure.

### Gaps
- No official count of Mandarin teachers abroad (global or by country) was found. China's CLEC/MOE releases give learner and country counts only. No ACTFL teacher census for Chinese was found.
- No US K-12 enrollment data newer than 2017 was found. No 2024-2026 national count of program closures was found.
- The exact MLA 2021 absolute Chinese and Japanese enrollment counts were not retrieved (the percentage changes are sourced). The figures must come from the MLA report or its Language Enrollment Database.
- UK 2024 and 2025 GCSE/A-level Chinese entries from JCQ/Ofqual were not retrieved. The gov.uk entries release for 2024-25 exists, but its subject table was not fetched.
- No Australian national (ACARA) Chinese enrollment data after 2016 was found.
- No primary NSA statement on STARTALK, and no sign of reinstatement, was found.

## 2. Heritage/weekend Chinese schools: scale and needs

### Takeaway
Heritage/weekend schools are the largest *countable* pool of Mandarin teachers abroad that I found. CSAUS alone reports 468 schools, 8,000+ teachers and 100,000+ students in the US, roughly a tenth of the Japan Foundation's worldwide Japanese-teacher count. The sector splits by origin into PRC-origin schools (simplified characters, CSAUS) and Taiwan-origin schools (traditional characters, NCACLS), and comparable UK and Australian figures are missing.

### Cited Findings
- CSAUS (Chinese School Association in the United States; schools founded mainly by PRC immigrants) reports 468 member schools in 50 states, 8,000+ teachers and 100,000+ students. It publishes its own "中文" textbook series (Jinan University Press), and its teacher resources cover textbooks, proficiency tests, materials and training. It ran 45 workshops/trainings in 2022 — [CSAUS](https://csaus.one/en/home-3/)
- Late-2023 CSAUS convention materials cite more than 460 schools and 100,000 students (another page says more than 400). An older Xinhua-sourced figure of about 400 schools and 150,000 students looks like an outlier. CSAUS was founded in 1994 with 16 schools in 14 states — [CSAUS](https://csaus.one/?p=5145); [CSAUS](https://csaus.one/?p=374); [BOL News/Xinhua](https://www.bolnews.com/?p=316841)
- NCACLS (National Council of Associations of Chinese Language Schools) was founded on April 15, 1994 and consists mainly of schools set up by immigrants from Taiwan, while CSAUS represents PRC-founded schools. NCACLS-affiliated schools had about 100,000 students as of early 2005 (dated) — [CLTA statistics page](https://clta-us.org/?p=273); [CAL Heritage](https://cal.org/heritagelanguageschoolsuschinese)
- All US Chinese heritage schools: about 82,675 students in 634 schools (1996), rising to about 140,000 by 2005 (CAL) — [CAL](https://cal.org/heritagelanguageschoolsuschinese)
- Taiwan's Ministry of Education runs an annual subsidy for overseas Chinese-language teachers (open dataset), evidence of ongoing Taiwan-side support for heritage teachers abroad — [data.gov.tw](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/42700)
- UK: Chinese complementary schools teach from nursery to A-level plus adult classes. The sector has shifted from mainly Cantonese to Mandarin. UKAPCE is the umbrella body — [London Review of Education 2014](https://journals.uclpress.co.uk/lre/article/2741/galley/17190/view); [Nottingham thesis](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/42429/). The British Council describes a "thriving" supplementary sector without figures — [British Council 2015](https://www.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/alcantara_full_report_jun15.pdf)
- Australia: at Sydney Chinese School the Mandarin share has risen to 60% as Cantonese declines — [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/this-language-has-a-rich-history-in-australia-but-its-at-risk-of-disappearing-forever/1e80480tn)
- Heritage students skew exam pipelines: the CSAUS/NCACLS pipeline overlaps with AP Chinese, and UK/Australian sources say heritage candidates dominate higher-level exams — [Tes](https://www.tes.com/news/native-chinese-pupils-may-be-skewing-stats-level-mandarin); [Crikey](https://stagecdn.crikey.com.au/?p=670399)

### Inferences
- A tool must support both simplified (CSAUS/PRC-origin, MEP, AP default) and traditional (NCACLS/Taiwan-origin) characters to serve US heritage schools fully. A simplified-only tool would leave out the Taiwan-origin segment.
- Weekend schools typically meet once a week, so most character practice happens at home. That makes printable 田字格/米字格 worksheets and share links parents can open without accounts especially valuable. Teachers are often part-time or parent volunteers with no budget, which fits a free tool. This is inferred from the school model and is not directly sourced here.
- Because CSAUS runs dozens of teacher trainings a year and publishes its own textbooks, distribution through association conferences or textbook-aligned character lists (e.g., the Jinan "中文" series) could be an efficient route. Lists keyed to those textbooks would be a strong hook.
- CSAUS's 8,000+ teachers alone equal about 10% of the 80,898 Japanese teachers abroad worldwide. US Chinese heritage schooling is therefore a meaningful segment in its own right.

### Gaps
- No current NCACLS school, teacher or student count was found (ncacls.org not fetched).
- No UK (UKAPCE) or Australian heritage-school census figures were found.
- No survey of heritage-teacher needs or pain points around character teaching was found. Needs above are inferred.

## 3. Tools teachers use for characters, complaints, and evidence for etymology/radical-based teaching

### Takeaway
The worksheet and stroke-order space is crowded with free-but-dated generators (Purple Culture, an2.net, Mandarin Minutes, open-source scripts) and one dominant paid teacher suite (Arch Chinese, about $100/yr), plus Pleco and Skritter for individual practice. Repeated criticisms are dated design, paywalls, missing pinyin or tone colors, and variation in stroke-order standards. Research supports radical/component instruction mainly for learning character *meaning*, with mixed results elsewhere, so origin stories should be framed as memory aids and not as a proven method.

### Cited Findings

**Tools in use**
- Arch Chinese, a teacher-oriented suite: worksheet maker, fill-in-the-blank maker, word-search generator, phrase dictation maker, flashcards, a radicals reference, stroke-order animations and radical breakdowns — [Arch Chinese worksheet maker](https://archchinese.com/chinese_worksheet_maker.html); [Arch features](https://archchinese.com/arch_features.html)
- Arch Chinese reviews: Maayot calls it a "fantastic resource hub" for Mandarin teachers. All Language Resources says the site doesn't seem to have put much effort into aesthetics but may suit teachers of school-age kids. Pricing is cited as about $49.99-$99.98 (possibly dated) or $100/yr with a limited free tier that "hits the paywall quickly" (the second figure is from a competitor's comparison) — [Maayot](https://www.maayot.com/blog/?p=2563); [All Language Resources](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/arch-chinese/); [Loqu8 Copyworks comparison 2026](https://copyworks.loqu8.com/blog/chinese-character-worksheet-generator-free.html)
- Free generators, per a 2026 comparison written by a competitor (Loqu8/Copyworks, so likely biased):
  - an2.net: free and basic, with no pinyin above characters, no tone colors and no stroke order, and a dated interface.
  - Purple Culture and Mandarin Minutes: free, no tone colors.
  - dohliam (GitHub): an open-source single HTML file, "very bare-bones."
  - Copyworks: free tier capped at 8 characters per sheet with a QR seal on every PDF. The paid tier ($29/yr or $49 lifetime) adds 田字格/米字格/回宫格 grids, HSK 1-9 lists, 33 textbooks, slides and left-handed mode.
  - Source: [Loqu8](https://copyworks.loqu8.com/blog/chinese-character-worksheet-generator-free.html)
- Pleco: the free version has animated stroke-order diagrams and a stroke-order flashcard test. A forum user noted that the only authoritative stroke order is "whatever my Chinese teacher says," pointing to regional (PRC/Taiwan/Japan) variation — [Pleco forums](https://www.plecoforums.com/threads/feature-req-testing-chinese-character-writing.3260)
- Skritter: paid, real-time stroke-order checking with spaced repetition, plus some printable material — [GoEast Mandarin tools](https://goeastmandarin.com/chinese-learning-tools/)
- Strokeorder.info is an animated stroke-order dictionary. Singapore's NIE keeps a curated list of Chinese ICT tools for teachers — [UAB eChinese directory](https://dtieao.uab.cat/txicc/echinese/en/details/strokeorderinfo); [NIE Singapore](https://ecltels.rdc.nie.edu.sg/lstelearn/ict-tools-aug23/chinese.html)
- HSK 3.0 requires handwriting from Band 1, which raises the importance of writing practice — [GoEast](https://goeastmandarin.com/chinese-learning-tools/)

**Evidence on radical/component/etymology instruction**
- A meta-analysis of 11 studies of radical-based instruction for non-native learners (Texas A&M dissertation) found a significant, large effect on producing character *meaning*, but no significant effect on radical awareness — [TAMU library record](https://libcat.library.tamu.edu/Record/in00005621282/Description) (from search snippet; full text not accessible, 403)
- Grouping characters that share a radical (University of Pittsburgh, Perfetti lab) gave beginners better recall and better radical generalization than spreading those characters out — [Xu et al., LRDC](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/perfettilab/pubpdfs/ChineseRadicalBasedGrouping.pdf)
- A correlational meta-analysis in Frontiers in Psychology (2022) covered 42 effect sizes from 1,103 participants. Orthographic awareness (r = 0.38), morphological awareness (r = 0.36) and phonological awareness (r = 0.41) were all moderately linked to Chinese word reading — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.783964/pdf)
- Counter-evidence:
  - With 39 L2 learners, radical awareness did not improve meaning retention for semantically *nontransparent* characters once character knowledge was controlled (SAGE Open, 2020) — [SAGE Open](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/xml/10.1177/2158244020969669)
  - In a 2022 study of 40 learners, giving radical and stroke information may have *interfered* with character learning — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/99d8c35127af48cca1dc223679d85ec7)
  - Author and year attributions for these two come from search summaries and were not checked against full texts.

### Inferences
- Teachers already have free stroke-order animation (Pleco, strokeorder.info) and free basic grids (Purple Culture, an2.net), so neither feature alone differentiates a new tool. The most defensible combination is likely projector-ready display, polished free 田字格/米字格 printing without paywalls or watermarks, simplified/traditional support, and no-signup links. This is inferred from the gaps competitors leave open.
- The evidence favors presenting origin/radical stories as a meaning aid and grouping characters by shared component (e.g., a "family" view). It does not support claiming radical instruction is universally superior, and the effect is weakest for characters whose components are not semantically transparent. Folk etymologies are a reputational risk with expert teachers; citing sources such as 说文解字 or modern paleography would help.
- Stroke-order variation (PRC GB standard vs. Taiwan MOE vs. Japanese) matters to teachers. Labeling which standard is shown would answer a known pain point.

### Gaps
- No teacher-forum or Reddit evidence on how teachers use Quizlet, Kahoot or YouTube stroke videos for characters, or on their complaints, surfaced.
- No independent classroom review of hanzigrids, Skritter for Schools/Classroom (pricing, adoption) or Purple Culture was found.
- No survey data on how many Mandarin teachers use worksheets or projectors, or how they rank character-teaching tools, was found.
- No study specifically on etymology/origin-story mnemonics (as opposed to radical awareness) with K-12 L2 learners was retrieved.

## 4. Constraints: student privacy, school approval lists, and politics around China-linked materials

### Takeaway
US districts increasingly require a signed Data Privacy Agreement (often through the Student Data Privacy Consortium) for any tool that collects student PII, and some block vendors who won't sign. A no-login tool that collects no student data may fall outside some of those requirements, though I found no source confirming this. Politically, scrutiny targets *Chinese-government-funded* programs (Confucius Classrooms, schools with CCP ties under Florida SB 846), so an independent, non-PRC-funded tool is on the safe side, but anything resembling PRC state materials would be a liability in some US states.

### Cited Findings
- Districts keep approved-software lists tied to the Student Data Privacy Consortium (SDPC) registry. Staff pull existing agreements from the SDPC Resource Registry or ask vendors to sign one, and districts can reuse agreements signed by other districts in their state — [San Ramon Valley USD](https://www.srvusd.k12.ca.us/Departments/Business-Services/Technology/Approved-Software/index.html); [search summary of district pages](https://www.wd7.org/departments/technology/student-data-privacy)
- New Hampshire: all software that collects or houses student PII must be approved and have a Data Privacy Agreement in place — [Newmarket NH](https://newmarket.k12.nh.us/sau31/technology/index)
- Illinois (SOPPA): one district says vendors that refuse to sign a DPA will not be used and may be blocked. Another notes a DPA is required only when the school shares student data with the vendor — [Moline SOPPA](https://sites.google.com/molineschools.org/tech-tools-for-learning/daily-operations-management/soppa-information); [OLCHS SOPPA](https://www.olchs.org/about-us/business-office/student-online-personal-protection-act-soppa)
- Florida SB 846 (May 2023) bars schools tied to a "foreign country of concern" (China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, Syria, Venezuela) from the state's school-choice scholarship programs. The state then suspended scholarships at four schools with alleged CCP ties. The same law bars public colleges from agreements or partnerships with foreign principals from those countries — [FLDOE](https://www.fldoe.org/newsroom/latest-news/icymi-florida-takes-action-against-four-florida-schools-with-ties-to-the-chinese-communist-party.stml); [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/florida-public-colleges-law-desantis-countries-concern-china/649843/)
- Federal: Rep. Aaron Bean introduced a bill requiring public K-12 schools to tell parents about dealings with "foreign governments of concern" (status not confirmed) — [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=765376)
- GAO found that 12 of the surveyed universities turned to Taiwanese entities after closing their Confucius Institutes, a sign of a shift toward non-PRC sources of Chinese teaching materials — [GAO-24-105981](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-105981)

### Inferences
- A no-signup, no-student-account design is a real procurement advantage: if no student PII is collected, many district DPA processes may not be triggered, and teachers can use the tool without IT approval. Districts that whitelist domains could still block it, so publishing a short privacy statement ("no student data, no accounts, no tracking"), and possibly registering in SDPC, would reduce friction.
- Making clear that the tool is independent and not funded by any government, and offering traditional characters and Taiwan stroke-order standards, would reassure districts and parents in politically sensitive US states. It would also serve the Taiwan-origin heritage segment.
- In the UK and Australia, the political constraint seems smaller. England's MEP is government-funded and run by UCL/British Council, but none of the sources here addressed UK or Australian school approval rules for tools.

### Gaps
- No primary source on how COPPA (including the FTC's recent COPPA Rule amendments) applies to no-login tools used by under-13s was retrieved. This needs direct FTC/legal review.
- No equivalents of UK GDPR/DfE or Australian state-department (e.g., NSW/VIC) approval lists for edtech were researched.
- No current list of US states that ban or restrict Confucius Classrooms specifically in K-12 was found (Florida's law covers scholarships and higher ed, and its K-12 public-school scope was not confirmed).

## 5. Comparison with Japanese teachers abroad and overall viability

### Takeaway
The Japanese baseline is well measured: 80,898 teachers at 19,344 institutions in 143 countries and regions (Japan Foundation 2024), with about 4 million learners. Mandarin has no comparable teacher census, but its learner base is claimed to be far larger (about 30M by Chinese government counts). The reachable non-China, non-Asia formal-school segments (US, UK, Australia) look similar in size to or smaller than their Japanese counterparts and are under political and funding pressure. The distinct opportunities are the large heritage/weekend school network (8,000+ CSAUS teachers in the US alone), the growing UK GCSE/MEP segment, and the fact that character writing is more central to Mandarin teaching (HSK 3.0 handwriting from Band 1). Mandarin teachers look like a viable *secondary* audience that needs simplified plus traditional support and heritage-school distribution.

### Cited Findings
- Japan Foundation 2024 survey: Japanese-language education in 143 countries and regions, 19,344 institutions, 80,898 teachers — [Japan Foundation 2024 survey](https://www.jpf.go.jp/e/project/japanese/survey/result/information.html); [JF press PDF](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/about/press/2025/dl/2025-031-03.pdf)
- Japanese learners: about 4 million in 143 countries and regions (MOFA) — [MOFA](https://www.mofa.go.jp/policy/culture/exchange/j_language/)
- US higher ed (MLA 2021): Japanese passed German in enrollment, and fewer Japanese programs were shrinking (48.8%) than Mandarin programs (61.2%) — [pinyin.info](https://pinyin.info/news/2023/us-postsecondary-enrollments-in-chinese-trending-down/)
- Mandarin learners abroad: more than 30 million (China-reported, 2023), against about 4M for Japanese — [rmzxw](https://www.rmzxw.com.cn/c/2023-12-20/3463132.shtml); [MOFA](https://www.mofa.go.jp/policy/culture/exchange/j_language/)
- Australia lists Mandarin and Japanese among its top three school languages — [California Insider](https://californiainsider.com/news/schools-could-adopt-mandarin-immersion-amid-falling-asian-language-enrolments-5956136)
- US heritage Chinese schools (CSAUS): 8,000+ teachers and 100,000+ students — [CSAUS](https://csaus.one/en/home-3/)

### Inferences
- China's 30M figure probably counts large numbers of learners in Southeast/South Asia, Korea, Japan and Africa who use national curricula and local materials (and Chinese-government-supplied resources). It does not translate one-to-one into English-speaking teachers looking for web tools. The English-medium Mandarin-teacher pool reachable by an English-UI tool is likely in the low tens of thousands (US K-12 + college + heritage schools + UK + Australia + international schools). That is the same order of magnitude as the English-speaking share of the 80,898 Japanese teachers. These are inferences, not sourced counts.
- Mandarin teachers likely need the product's specific features (printable 田字格/米字格, animated stroke order, projector mode) *more* than Japanese teachers do, because Mandarin teaching rests almost entirely on characters (no kana stage) and heritage weekend schools rely heavily on take-home handwriting sheets. Per teacher, demand intensity is plausibly higher than for Japanese.
- Risks:
  1. A crowded free-tool field (Pleco, Purple Culture, an2.net) plus an entrenched paid suite (Arch Chinese).
  2. Shrinking US/Australian formal programs and lost federal subsidies (STARTALK, Confucius).
  3. The need for two scripts and two stroke-order standards.
  4. Political sensitivity in some US states.
  5. Mixed evidence that etymology improves outcomes beyond meaning recall.
- Suggested beachheads, ranked by evidence of size and accessibility:
  1. US heritage schools through CSAUS/NCACLS conferences.
  2. England MEP schools through UCL IOE.
  3. AP/IB teachers.
  4. University instructors (declining).

### Gaps
- No like-for-like teacher count for Mandarin abroad exists in the sources found, so a direct teacher-to-teacher comparison with the Japan Foundation's 80,898 is not possible.
- AP Japanese candidate numbers and the 2017 US K-12 Japanese enrollment were not retrieved for a same-source comparison with AP Chinese (18,312) and K-12 Chinese (227,086).
- The Japan Foundation 2024 regional breakdown (e.g., US, UK, Australia teacher counts) was not fetched, so country-level comparisons are missing.
