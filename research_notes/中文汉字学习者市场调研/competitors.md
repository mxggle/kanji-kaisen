Notes on method and dating:
- "Seen 2026-10-31" means I read the price on the vendor's own page or App Store listing on that date. Other prices come from third-party sources and carry those sources' dates. Third-party prices are often stale or regional.

## Q1. Who serves Mandarin learners with etymology, radicals and visual storytelling: audience, method, price, platform, design, sentiment, and whether they show ancient forms

### Takeaway
The market falls into five groups:
1. Rigorous but paid and mobile-locked etymology: Outlier, sold inside Pleco.
2. Free but scholarly, dense or dated reference sites: 小学堂, Wiktionary, hanziyuan.net, zdic, Zhongwen.com, YellowBridge, HanziCraft.
3. Paid SRS and mnemonic courses that use invented stories rather than real etymology: HanziHero, Mandarin Blueprint, Skritter's hints.
4. Beautiful but folk-pictographic consumer apps: Chineasy.
5. A growing tail of small freemium mobile apps from 2021–2026 that add illustrations and ancient forms: Hanly, InkSeal, Wen, Chinese Etymology and Origins.

Dong Chinese's free CC BY-SA character wiki comes closest to "free, web, rigorous, with ancient forms", but it is a utilitarian reference attached to a paid course.

### Inferences
- **The only premium, research-grade etymology product aimed at learners is Outlier.**
  - Its distribution is mobile-only and lives inside third-party apps: Pleco, and Yomiwa for kanji.
  - Outlier's own statement of priorities says courses are now its revenue engine.
  - Expert entries have grown slowly (135 → about 410 in six years).
  - Together these leave room for a web-first, visual presentation of rigorous etymology.
- **Free rigorous sources exist** (小学堂, Wiktionary, the Dong Chinese wiki, hanziyuan.net), but they are references, not designed experiences.
  - None groups characters by theme (nature, body, tools), animates oracle → bronze → seal → modern as a sequence, or produces shareable cards.
  - This is my judgement from the feature descriptions above. I did not find a source that rates their design.
- **The newcomer wave is mobile-native, small (4 to 409 ratings) and freemium:** Hanly, InkSeal, Wen.
  - It shows demand for "illustrated story + ancient form + SRS".
  - None of these apps runs on the web or targets teachers.

  - Skritter: $99.99/year

## Q2. Which treat etymology rigorously vs. folk stories, and how do learners react?

### Takeaway
Rigorous: Outlier, 小学堂, the Dong Chinese wiki (sourced, with dated script images), and Wiktionary (traditional sources). Folk, invented or mnemonic: Chineasy, HanziHero, Mandarin Blueprint, Zizzle, and Zhongwen.com (traditional Shuowen-based, self-described as a learning tool). hanziyuan.net has the richest glyph collection but makes unsourced, single-author claims.

Experts strongly criticise folk pictography (Chineasy). Practitioners such as Hacking Chinese and Outlier accept mnemonics as long as they respect real component function, especially sound components. Mass-market users still rate the folk-visual apps highly: Chineasy has 4.8★.

### Cited Findings
- **Victor Mair on Chineasy:**
  - Chineasy's visual "stories" are arbitrary (e.g. its 划 explanation). It ignores pronunciation.
  - He calls it a business that depends on customers' ignorance. He also criticised Babel magazine for printing ShaoLan's piece uncritically.
  - [Language Log, 2014-08-14](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=14051)
  - His key line: "Nobody can even begin to learn Chinese with Chineasy."
- Language Log also ran an earlier post titled "Chineasy? Not" — [Language Log](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=11109)
- Hacking Chinese lists Olle Linge's critique "Chineasy? Not (About what's wrong with Hsueh ShaoLan's Chineasy)". I could not fetch the article. — [Hacking Chinese tag page](https://hackingchinese.com/tag/chineasy)
- **Mass-market reaction to Chineasy:** high store ratings (4.8★, thousands of reviews), but reviewers agree it is beginner-only. Critics point to the mix of simplified and traditional characters and its pictograph-heavy teaching. — [AppPricingLab](https://www.apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1318941705); [GoEast](https://goeastmandarin.com/can-you-learn-chinese-characters-from-chineasy/); [alternativeto](https://alternativeto.net/software/chineasy/about)
- **Outlier:**
  - Mair praises it for avoiding folk etymologies and for tracing oracle bone → seal forms — [Language Log 2018](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=40329)
  - Hacking Chinese says its creator supports mnemonics when they are grounded in correct character structure. The review counts the weakness of mnemonic systems that ignore functional components against those systems. — [Hacking Chinese 2020](https://www.hackingchinese.com/review-the-outlier-linguistics-dictionary-of-chinese-characters-with-discount-code/)
- **Hacking Chinese's stance:**
  - Learners need not master true etymology but should look it up in case it helps.
  - Use mnemonics selectively.
  - Ignoring how components combine hurts retention.
  - Do not force a meaning onto every component (e.g. 妈 is not "woman + horse"; 马 is phonetic).
  - [Hacking Chinese: compound characters](https://www.hackingchinese.com/the-building-blocks-of-chinese-part-4-learning-and-remembering-compound-characters/); [Hacking Chinese etymology tag](https://www.hackingchinese.com/tag/etymology/)
- **Linge:** when inventing memory aids, the goal is memorability, not true etymology, which is usually partly phonetic anyway — [Hacking Chinese: "What's in a name"](https://www.hackingchinese.com/whats-name-hacking-chinese/)
- **Expert advice on sources (Outlier staff, Aug 2023):**
  - 說文新證 is excellent.
  - 字源 varies by entry author.
  - 汉字源流字典 forces meaning onto sound components and is not recommended.
  - The 以观书法 app is a convenient way to access these references.
  - Learners noted these sources are Chinese-only, which makes them hard to share with learners.
  - [Chinese-Forums](https://chinese-forums.com/forums/topic/62958-on-the-current-state-of-outlier-and-etymology-dictionaries)
- **Zhongwen.com:** etymologies follow traditional scholarship. Harbaugh himself warns not to confuse it with a scholarly guide. Archaeology now supplies earlier forms than the Shuowen. — [Grainger review](https://grainger.de/dbe/sbs/chineseref003.html)
- **hanziyuan.net:** Sears claims most existing explanations are incorrect or doubtful. Entries carry no per-character citations, but a GitHub "Report issue" link exists. — [hanziyuan.net](https://hanziyuan.net/)
- **Mnemonic-course learner reactions:**
  - HanziHero users dislike learning invented names for sounds — [WaniKani forum](https://community.wanikani.com/t/hanzihero-the-chinese-wanikani/68648)
  - Mandarin Blueprint users call the system effective but initially confusing, with sets getting mixed up — [FluentU](https://www.fluentu.com/blog/reviews/mandarin-blueprint/)
- **Reddit (search snippet only):** a heated r/ChineseLanguage thread disputes Outlier's accuracy versus Dong Chinese. Some commenters see praise for Outlier from other learning companies as sponsorship-driven. — [search summary of r/ChineseLanguage](https://redlib.andus.dev/r/ChineseLanguage/comments/1g0eltg/stop_using_radicals_they_lost_their_purpose_and)
- **Hybrid positioning exists:** HanziHack says its method is "historically informed" but practical, not a formal etymology dictionary — [HanziHack FAQ](https://sites.google.com/hanzihack.com/web/faq-english)

### Inferences
- The engaged-learner community (Hacking Chinese, Chinese-Forums, Language Log readers) rewards accuracy and penalises folk pictography. The mass market rewards visual charm.
- A product that is both beautiful and rigorous would sit in the gap between Chineasy and Outlier. Its credibility hooks would be dated, sourced script images (the Dong Chinese pattern), honest treatment of phonetic components, and a separation between a mnemonic or story layer and an etymology layer.
- Sharing a "story layer" across Chinese and Japanese is risky with this audience if stories are presented as etymology. Labelling stories as mnemonics and etymology as sourced fact would match what the critics ask for.

### Gaps
- I could not read Olle Linge's "Chineasy? Not" article or the full Reddit threads.
- No quantitative study of learner preference (rigorous vs folk) was found.

## Q3. Is there any product covering both Japanese kanji and Chinese hanzi with a shared etymology layer? How is it received?

### Takeaway
Outlier is the only established vendor with parallel Chinese and Japanese etymology dictionaries built on the same method. They are separate purchases inside separate host apps (Pleco and Yomiwa), with a separate $99 Kanji Masterclass. Scribe Origins (iPad) covers both scripts through shared primitives, not etymology. SmartHanzi for Kanjis brings scholarly kanji etymology to an app. An open-source dual-mode web dictionary (hanzi-etymology-dict) exists but is very early. Skritter covers both languages for handwriting only. Reception data for cross-language products is thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Outlier Kanji Dictionary:**
  - Essentials at $29.99 and an Outlier Kanji Masterclass at $99 are listed on the 2026 homepage — [outlier-linguistics.com](https://outlier-linguistics.com/)
  - The kanji dictionary is an add-on for the free Yomiwa app (iOS/Android), with Essentials and Expert tiers. Yomiwa's App Store listing shows Outlier IAPs.
  - Product pages are inconsistent: one shows Essentials as sold out, and one says to activate via Pleco.
  - [Search summary of Outlier kanji pages](https://www.outlier-linguistics.com/products/outlier-kanji-dictionary-essentials-edition); [Outlier Kanji Expert page](https://www.outlier-linguistics.com/products/outlier-kanji-dictionary-expert-edition)
- **Crowdfunding history:** a WaniKani forum thread covers the Kickstarter launch. The search summary says it was a relaunch after a failed funding attempt; I did not verify this. — [WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/kickstarter-for-outlier-kanji-dictionary-is-now-live/31061?page=2)
- **Use for teaching:** a WaniKani forum thread discusses using Outlier's kanji dictionary and etymology for teaching — [WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/outlier-kanji-dictionary-and-etymology-for-teaching/31116)
- **Scribe Origins (iPad):** 4,800 kanji and hanzi taught together through shared primitives (e.g. 親 = 見 + 立 + 木), with "pseudo-primitive" breakdowns — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id616678052)
- **SmartHanzi for Kanjis:** kanji etymology from Howell & Morimoto's *Etymological Dictionary of Han/Chinese Characters*. A forum post says it also cites Wieger — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1535211451); [Chinese-Forums](https://chinese-forums.com/forums/topic/57872-chinese-etymology-in-smarthanzi)
- **hanzi-etymology-dict:**
  - A dual Chinese/Japanese web view in which Japanese etymology currently reuses the Chinese data.
  - 13,108 kanji; 6 GitHub stars.
  - [GitHub](https://github.com/lbm364dl/hanzi-etymology-dict)
- **Skritter:** covers Chinese and Japanese handwriting, with no etymology — [StudyCLI](https://studycli.org/what-is-skritter)
- **Wiktionary:** its Japanese kanji sections do not carry the Chinese glyph-origin content, so the two layers are not shared there — [Wiktionary 水](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%B0%B4)
- A general search found no app built specifically around a shared Han-character etymology layer for both languages — [search summary](https://www.maayot.com/blog/hanzi-vs-kanji/)

### Inferences
- A product with a shared origin layer (oracle bone → seal is common to both scripts) plus language-specific layers would be novel in a polished, web-first form. The language-specific layers would cover modern form (simplified, traditional or shinjitai), readings and vocabulary.
- Outlier already validates demand on both sides, but it splits the experience across two third-party mobile apps.
- The shared layer must handle where the scripts diverge: simplified vs shinjitai forms (e.g. 体/體, 広/廣) and kokuji. Otherwise "shared" stories will mislead one audience.

### Gaps
- I found no reviews, ratings or sales data for the Outlier Kanji Dictionary or Scribe Origins.
- The current Yomiwa add-on prices could not be verified.

## Q4. Gaps: free + beautiful + web + radical- and theme-organised + origin animation + shareable cards; teacher features for Mandarin classes

### Takeaway
No product found combines all six. Each requirement has partial coverage:
- **Free + web + rigorous ancient forms:** the Dong Chinese wiki, 小学堂, Wiktionary and hanziyuan.net. All are reference-style, and none has theme organisation or animated evolution.
- **Beautiful visuals:** Chineasy, which is paid and folk. Also InkSeal and Hanly, which are mobile freemium.
- **Stroke animation:** commoditised via MIT-licensed Hanzi Writer and Make Me a Hanzi data.
- **Oracle → bronze → seal → modern as an animated morph:** I found no product that does this. A 2020 AR card set from Richard Sears appears to be the nearest attempt.
- **Teacher tooling:** sparse and paid: Skritter Classroom (unpriced), Arch Chinese worksheets ($49.99/6 months and up), and Chineasy's physical materials.

### Cited Findings
- **Free web references with ancient forms:**
  - Dong Chinese wiki (CC BY-SA, Academia Sinica images, four scripts) — [Dong Chinese wiki](https://www.dong-chinese.com/wiki/%E6%B0%B4)
  - 小学堂 (220k+ glyphs, evolution subsystem) — [小学堂 PDF](https://xiaoxue.iis.sinica.edu.tw/yanbian/Content/Files/yanbian-Get_Started.pdf)
  - hanziyuan.net (100k forms, free, dated) — [hanziyuan.net](https://hanziyuan.net/)
  - Wiktionary — [Wiktionary 水](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%B0%B4)
- **Dong Chinese coverage:** origin explanations cover the 1,000 most frequent characters. The main product is a paid course from $9.99/month. — [dong-chinese.com](https://www.dong-chinese.com/); [pricing](https://www.dong-chinese.com/pricing)
- **Animated evolution:** the closest is the 2020 AR "Chinese Character Origin Cards", 108 characters with animation, co-developed by Richard Sears and Shiwangme. Current availability is unknown. — [China Daily 2020](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202008/28/WS5f486552a310675eafc56121.html)
- **Hanly** has "evolution" pages from ancient pictograph to modern form inside a mobile app — [hanlyapp.com](https://www.hanlyapp.com/)
- **Stroke-order animation is freely available to build on:** Hanzi Writer is MIT; its data is under the Arphic Public License — [GitHub hanzi-writer](https://github.com/chanind/hanzi-writer)
- **Teacher features:**
  - Skritter Classroom: student accounts, class licence codes, settings, progress tracking — [Skritter docs](https://docs.skritter.com/article/61-what-is-skritter-classroom)
  - Arch Chinese: worksheet generator behind membership — [Arch Chinese membership](https://archchinese.com/arch_membership.html)
  - Chineasy: Tiles teacher's guide, colouring book, newsletter, with no dashboard or licences shown — [chineasy.com/educator](https://chineasy.com/educator)
  - Outlier's Expert entries are considered useful for teachers — [Hacking Chinese 2020](https://www.hackingchinese.com/review-the-outlier-linguistics-dictionary-of-chinese-characters-with-discount-code/)
- **Why free rigorous sources are hard for teachers:** the scholarly references (說文新證, 字源) are Chinese-only, which makes them hard to share with learners — [Chinese-Forums 2023](https://chinese-forums.com/forums/topic/62958-on-the-current-state-of-outlier-and-etymology-dictionaries)
- **Free worksheet generators exist:** for example an2.net/zim, with 田字格/米字格 grids and stroke order — [DigMandarin 2024](https://www.digmandarin.com/resources-for-learning-chinese-characters.html)

### Inferences
- **Open lanes:**
  - A free, web-first, well-designed atlas organised by radical and by theme.
  - Each character with a sourced, dated sequence: oracle bone → bronze → seal → clerical → modern, animated as a morph or timeline.
  - Stroke-order animation (Hanzi Writer).
  - An honest "how it works" panel that separates semantic and phonetic components.
  - Shareable image cards.
  - Nothing found combines all of these.
- **Teacher features that appear unaddressed:**
  - A projector or presentation mode for a themed set (e.g. "body" characters).
  - Printable or shareable card decks with ancient forms.
  - Class links that need no student accounts.
  - These matter because the paid incumbents focus on drilling (Skritter) or worksheets (Arch Chinese).
- **Defensibility and risk:**
  - Ancient-form imagery has to come from licensable sources. Dong Chinese's wiki is CC BY-SA (share-alike obligations). Academia Sinica images carry their own terms. hanziyuan.net is unlicensed for reuse as far as I found.
  - Outlier licenses its data commercially and refuses to open-source it.
  - Image licensing will probably be the main constraint on "beautiful + rigorous + free".
- **Competitive threats:**
  - Dong Chinese could polish its wiki.
  - Hanly, the strongest newcomer by rating count (409 ratings, 4.9★), could add a web version.
  - Outlier could launch a web app.
  - I saw no public signal of any of these.

### Gaps
- **Licence terms:** I did not verify the terms for Academia Sinica 小学堂 glyph images, or the Make Me a Hanzi repository licence file directly.
- **Teacher demand:** I found no data on how many Mandarin teachers use etymology tools, or what they would pay.
- **Product Hunt:** search returned no Product Hunt pages, so 2024–2026 launches there are unconfirmed.
