# Competitive Landscape of Kanji Learning Tools (as of 2026-10-31)

Unless noted otherwise, prices were seen on 2026-10-31 and are in USD. Many prices come from secondary or undated sources, and those are marked. The search tool did not return Reddit threads directly. Reddit sentiment is therefore represented only where a blog or forum quoted it; most user voice here comes from the WaniKani and Bunpro community forums, app store aggregators and blog reviews.

## Q1. What is each tool's audience, model, price, platform and review sentiment?

### Takeaway
The market splits four ways. There are paid, structured mnemonic and SRS courses (WaniKani, Bunpro, Skritter, Satori Reader, Migaku). There are free or cheap but dated or DIY tools (Anki, Kanji Koohii, Kanji Damage, Kanji alive, Jisho, Takoboto). There are well-rated mobile-only apps (Kanji Study on Android, Ringotan, Kanji Dojo, Kanji Garden). And there are general courses with weak kanji handling (Duolingo, Renshuu as an all-rounder). No single free tool combines radical stories, stroke order, polished design and a web-first experience.

### Cited Findings

**WaniKani (Tofugu LLC)**
- The official price is $9/month, $89/year or $299 lifetime, one-time, with tax or VAT possibly added. The footer reads "© Tofugu LLC 2026". Seen 2026-10-31. — [WaniKani Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.wanikani.com/getting-started/account-and-membership/payment-and-billing/wanikani-premium-membership/)
- WaniKani runs one sale a year with $100 off Lifetime. A past sale price was US$199 (was $299). Users say the sale usually comes around Christmas. — [wanikani.com/sale](https://wanikani.com/sale); [OzBargain](https://www.ozbargain.com.au/tag/japanese-language); [WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/wanikani-lifetime-subscription-pricing/62156)
- The first 3 levels are free. The method teaches radicals, then kanji, then vocabulary, using mnemonics and SRS, and it runs on web, iOS and Android. Reviewers credit its clear structure, memorable mnemonics and "no planning needed". Weaknesses: it covers reading recognition only, with no handwriting, and its pacing is strict. Article last updated 2026-08-14. — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)
- It targets about 1,700 kanji and about 5,000 vocabulary words over roughly 1 to 2 years. A roundup warns that "if you stop studying for a while, the review backlog grows fast." — [alternativeto / roundup summary](https://alternativeto.net/software/wanikani/about)
- One review criticizes the slow early levels. It says WaniKani covers about a quarter of what JLPT prep needs, and that each level takes a minimum of about 7 days because of gating. It also notes that for people who quit partway, lifetime can cost more than paying monthly for the months actually used. — [JLPT Lord WaniKani review](https://jlptlord.com/blog/wanikani-review)
- An ecosystem of third-party apps exists to fix pain points. Juken is a "minimalistic review app for WaniKani optimized for speed". Gakugame is a companion app built around daily review limits; its developer says he "almost quit three times" because of the review pile. — [Juken GitHub](https://github.com/oguzgelal/juken/blob/master/README.md); [Gakugame App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6755092872)

**Anki (with RTK, Kanji Koohii and Kanji Damage decks)**
- Anki is free on desktop, web and Android. iOS is a one-time purchase of about $25. It is a flashcard SRS that relies on community decks. Weaknesses: a plain look, technical setup, and no grammar teaching. Source dated 2026-08. — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)
- Anki is free and open source. — [alternativeto](https://alternativeto.net/software/kanji-koohii/?p=6)

**Kanji Koohii**
- Kanji Koohii is free ("It's free!") and has run since 2006, built by one developer in Belgium. It offers user-written and community-shared mnemonic stories, RTK flashcards with SRS, and a "smart dictionary" that highlights words using only kanji the learner has already studied. It is meant to be used alongside Heisig's *Remembering the Kanji*. — [kanji.koohii.com](https://kanji.koohii.com/)
- An official RTK companion app covers over 3,000 kanji, with stroke-order animations and handwriting practice. — [App Store: Remembering the Kanji](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/id424471278)

**Kanji Damage**
- Kanji Damage is free and covers about 1,700 kanji with mnemonics. Its tone is deliberately crude, and a reviewer calls it NSFW. It is static web pages rather than an interactive tool, with an official Anki deck for review. — [Tofugu resource database](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-learning-resources-database/kanji-damage/)

**Kanji alive**
- Kanji alive is a free web app for reading and writing kanji, with a free public API and source code on GitHub. Its creators say it "is not designed to be used as a dictionary". Its dataset covers 1,235 kanji and 247 radicals, with stroke-order media. — [kanjialive.com](https://kanjialive.com/); [GitHub kanji-data-media](https://github.com/kanjialive/kanji-data-media/blob/master/README.md)

**Jisho**
- Jisho is a free browser-based Japanese-English dictionary for instant lookups, called "superb" in one roundup. It is a reference tool with no study path. — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)

**Takoboto**
- Takoboto is a free dictionary and study tool with flashcards, study lists, kana tables and conjugation tables. Its flashcards are "not as interactive as those in Kanji Study", but it exports to AnkiDroid more cleanly. — [All Language Resources](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/takoboto/)

**Japanese Kanji Study (Chase Colburn / mindtwisted)**
- It offers SRS, flashcards, multiple-choice quizzes, writing challenges, search and custom sets. It is free with in-app purchases; one roundup describes a one-time upgrade that unlocks all levels and custom sets. — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.mindtwisted.kanjistudy); [alternativeto](https://alternativeto.net/software/japanese-kanji-study)
- Its Google Play rating is about 4.85 from about 61.1K ratings, per an aggregator snapshot from around May 2026; an earlier snapshot showed about 54.6K ratings. — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.mindtwisted.kanjistudy); [BlueStacks listing](https://www.bluestacks.com/apps/education/japanese-kanji-study-kanji-learning-on-pc.html)
- A roundup says it is Android-only. Note that the assignment brief said "Android/iOS"; I could not confirm an iOS version. — [alternativeto](https://alternativeto.net/software/japanese-kanji-study)

**Renshuu**
- Renshuu is free, with Pro from about $6.99/month and a lifetime option (secondary source, dated 2026-08). It runs on web, iOS and Android. It is an all-in-one SRS covering vocabulary, grammar and kanji, with textbook-aligned schedules. Strengths: a complete free tier, high customizability and a warm community. Weaknesses: a "busy interface with a learning curve", and some visual learners find it "less polished". Reddit reportedly calls it "the best-kept secret in the hobby." — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)

**Skritter (Japanese)**
- Skritter is a handwriting-first SRS with handwriting recognition. One comparison table lists it at $14.99 for 1 month, $59.99 for 6 months and $99.99 for 1 year, though those figures may be for the Chinese version and are not dated. An older review cites $9.95/month. The app gives a free week, then a subscription. — [writtenchinese.com](https://www.writtenchinese.com/the-two-white-chicks-review-skritter/); [gakuran.com](https://gakuran.com/skritter-for-writing-japanese-review/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1370893674)
- Reviewers call it "the best ... character training app with handwriting recognition" but "slightly overpriced". Users praise the writing animation and the erase-and-retry flow. Complaints centre on cost, plus sync and canvas issues. — [gakuran.com](https://gakuran.com/skritter-for-writing-japanese-review/); [discoverdiscomfort 2026 review](https://discoverdiscomfort.com/skritter-review-learn-chinese-characters/); [marlvel.ai aggregate (low-confidence, automated)](https://marlvel.ai/apps/skritter-write-japanese/reviews)

**Ringotan**
- Ringotan is a free, ad-free kanji-writing SRS for iOS and Android. It is often paired with WaniKani and covers WaniKani, JLPT and textbook lists. App Store rating: 4.9 from about 10 ratings. Google Play has about 1.37K reviews. Complaints: crashes, getting stuck on a correctly drawn kanji, too many repetitions of new kanji (later fixed with a daily limit), weak phonetic hints at advanced levels, and missing notifications and dark mode. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.Ringotan); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ringotan/id1633001064); [WaniKani community thread](https://community.wanikani.com/t/ringotan-free-app-for-learning-how-to-write-kanji/51702)

**Kanji Garden**
- Kanji Garden is a free, browser-based kanji SRS with heavy customization: enter an email and start studying. It adapts to the learner's level, including letting them skip past kanji they already know. The iOS app is free, rated about 4.5 to 4.7. — [Tofugu resource database](https://tofugu.com/japanese-learning-resources-database/kanji-garden); [AppAdvice](https://appadvice.com/app/kanji-garden-japanese/1338967114)

**Kanji Dojo (newer, open source)**
- Kanji Dojo is a free, GPL-3.0 app teaching writing and stroke order for more than 6,000 kana and kanji. It works offline, has no ads, and runs on Android, F-Droid, iOS and iPad (App Store id 6745169386, so 2025-era), and desktop (Mac, Windows, Linux). The iOS listing says there is no paid content, while AppGoblin shows IAP on Android. — [GitHub](https://github.com/SYtor/Kanji-Dojo); [alternativeto](https://alternativeto.net/software/kanji-dojo/about); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/ua.syt0r.kanji)

**Satori Reader**
- Satori Reader offers graded reading with furigana and explanations, not kanji drills. US App Store price: $8.99/month or $89.99/year, first month free. EU price is about €9.49/month. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/satori-reader/id1382950847)

**Duolingo (kanji handling)**
- Duolingo is free with ads, or about $7/month for Super. It has "weak kanji teaching", and users can tap through without understanding. — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)
- After the 2023 course overhaul, learners complained that kanji had been removed. Duolingo staff said kanji were spread across the course to match Japan Foundation and JLPT standards. Since then it has added kanji tracing, freehand writing and component exercises on mobile, per a blog updated 2026-08. Complaints from 2024 onward: kanji are introduced slowly, a learner cannot pick a specific character to trace, and there is no furigana. — [r/duolingo staff retro (mirror)](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/duolingo/comments/15i3lwb/a_retro_on_the_japanese_updates_and_some_notes); [LingoDeer blog](https://blog.lingodeer.com/duolingo-japanese-review/); [r/LearnJapanese 2023 thread (mirror)](https://reddit.rtrace.io/r/LearnJapanese/comments/139pg80/duolingo_just_ruined_their_japanese_course/jj3pk28/?context=3)
- In April 2026 Duolingo said it is adding "catch-up lessons" after complaints about course updates. — [PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/04/02/duolingo-adding-catch-up-lessons-after-course-update-complaints/)

**Bunpro**
- Bunpro is a grammar SRS, with vocabulary decks added later. It is not a kanji tool, but WaniKani users often pair it. The official site lists $5/month and $150 lifetime; community posts give $50/year. In-app prices are higher: $6.49/month, $64.99/year, $194.99 lifetime. — [bunpro.jp/pricing](https://bunpro.jp/pricing); [Bunpro community](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/yearly-or-lifetime/41188); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1615337616)
- Weakness: it is "not a full course". — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)

**Migaku**
- The pricing page was not machine-readable. The homepage showed a Halloween promo of 52% off the lifetime plan, with no price shown, on 2026-10-31. — [migaku.com/pricing](https://migaku.com/pricing)

**2024–2026 newcomers**
- Kanaji is a simple iOS kanji-writing app. It launched on Product Hunt on 2025-11-29 and finished #9 with 104 upvotes. — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/kanaji); [hunted.space](https://hunted.space/dashboard/kanaji/launches/kanaji)
- Ziyo is a kanji dictionary and search engine, featured on Product Hunt on 2025-10-04 with 112 votes. — [Product Hunt leaderboard 2025-10-04](https://www.producthunt.com/leaderboard/daily/2025/10/4)
- Kann (Android, 2025) is a vocabulary app; its maker says he mastered N5 kanji in about 3 weeks while building it. — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/kann-for-android?launch=kann-for-android)
- Kanji Native is a free SRS kanji app from a startup covered by the EU-Japan Centre; its launch date could not be confirmed. — [EU-Japan Centre](https://www.eu-japan.eu/news/start-innovating-new-kanji-app)

### Inferences
- Price anchors: a structured kanji course costs about $9/month or $89–99/year (WaniKani, Skritter, Satori). Lifetime plans cost $150–$300. Free options are either DIY (Anki), aging single-maintainer sites (Koohii since 2006, Kanji alive with 1,235 kanji), or mobile-only apps.
- Kanji Study's roughly 61K ratings at 4.85 suggest a polished, low-cost native app can win at scale without a brand budget. It is Android-centric, though, which leaves iOS and web users without the equivalent.
- WaniKani is the reference product for radical mnemonics but has no writing. Writing tools such as Ringotan, Kanji Dojo and Skritter have no mnemonic or story layer. Kanji Kaisen's combination of radical stories and stroke order sits between these two clusters.

### Gaps
- MOJi (MOJi辞書/MOJi Kanji): no data on price, ratings or method was found.
- Migaku and Renshuu prices could not be read from the official pages. The Renshuu figure (about $6.99/month) is secondary.
- No current Trustpilot data was found for any tool.
- Direct Reddit "which kanji app" threads were not returned by search, so Reddit sentiment comes from secondhand quotes only.
- WaniKani's App Store and Google Play rating was not retrieved.

## Q2. What complaints recur across the category?

### Takeaway
The most-cited pains are: review-pile burnout and rigid level gating (WaniKani), cost and subscription fatigue (WaniKani, Skritter), no writing practice in mnemonic tools, dated or busy UI in free tools (Anki, Renshuu), and fragmentation that forces learners to stack 2 to 4 tools.

### Cited Findings
- **Review-pile burnout:** WaniKani's backlog "grows fast" after a break. A whole app category (Juken, Gakugame) exists to make reviews faster or capped, and Gakugame's developer "almost quit three times". — [alternativeto](https://alternativeto.net/software/wanikani/about); [Gakugame](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6755092872); [Juken](https://github.com/oguzgelal/juken/blob/master/README.md)
- **Rigid gating and slow pace:** WaniKani needs at least about 7 days per level, and its early levels are slow. — [JLPT Lord](https://jlptlord.com/blog/wanikani-review); [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/) ("strict pacing")
- **Paywalls and price:** Skritter's subscription is seen as "prohibitively expensive relative to local market purchasing power" (automated aggregate, low confidence). Reviewers call Skritter "slightly overpriced". A WaniKani review says lifetime can lose money for people who quit. — [marlvel.ai](https://marlvel.ai/apps/skritter-write-japanese/reviews); [gakuran](https://gakuran.com/skritter-for-writing-japanese-review/); [JLPT Lord](https://jlptlord.com/blog/wanikani-review)
- **No writing practice:** WaniKani covers reading recognition only, with no handwriting. — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)
- **Ugly, dated or busy UI:** Anki has a "plain look" and "technical setup". Renshuu has a "busy interface with a learning curve" and is "less polished". Kanji Damage is crude and NSFW, which rules it out for schools. — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/); [Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-learning-resources-database/kanji-damage/)
- **Shallow kanji in general apps:** Duolingo introduces kanji slowly, does not let learners pick which characters to practise, and has no furigana. — [LingoDeer blog](https://blog.lingodeer.com/duolingo-japanese-review/)
- **Bugs and missing basics in indie apps:** Ringotan has had crashes, false negatives on drawn kanji, and no dark mode or notifications. — [WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/ringotan-free-app-for-learning-how-to-write-kanji/51702)
- **Tool stacking:** A 2026 roundup advises pairing one flashcard app with one grammar tool, plus reading and listening. Ringotan is popular specifically as a writing add-on to WaniKani. — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/); [WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/ringotan-free-app-for-learning-how-to-write-kanji/51702)

### Inferences
- A tool that has no review debt (an atlas you browse, not a queue you owe), is free, and combines story with stroke order addresses three of the top complaints at once.
- Poor mobile web is implied rather than directly evidenced: most writing tools are native-only (Kanji Study, Ringotan, Kanaji), and the web tools (Koohii, Kanji alive) are dated.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative complaint share, such as the percentage of 1-star reviews by theme, for any app.
- No source directly complained about "poor mobile web"; this remains an inference.

## Q3. Which tools have strong design or brand, and does design drive adoption?

### Takeaway
WaniKani has the strongest brand: the Tofugu ecosystem, a crabigator mascot, and mnemonic voice. Its brand and structure are what reviewers praise, and it is the default recommendation. The free alternatives are consistently described as plain or busy. Evidence that design itself drives word of mouth is circumstantial.

### Cited Findings
- WaniKani is the most common pick, "the most popular kanji app for good reason", praised for clear structure and memorable mnemonics. — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)
- Renshuu is "less polished" for visual learners even though it is the "best free all-rounder". Anki has a "plain look". — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)
- Skritter's writing animation and the satisfying erase-and-retry flow are singled out as reasons users enjoy it. — [discoverdiscomfort 2026](https://discoverdiscomfort.com/skritter-review-learn-chinese-characters/); [mezzoguild](https://mezzoguild.com/skritter-review)
- Small design-led launches get modest Product Hunt traction: Kanaji (104 upvotes, #9) and Ziyo (112 votes). — [Product Hunt Kanaji](https://www.producthunt.com/products/kanaji); [PH 2025-10-04](https://www.producthunt.com/leaderboard/daily/2025/10/4)

### Inferences
- The pattern "free and powerful but ugly" (Anki, Renshuu, Koohii) versus "beautiful and branded but paid" (WaniKani) suggests that a free and beautiful product is an open position.
- WaniKani's lead likely comes from brand voice and a no-planning structure as much as from visual design. Kanji Kaisen's radical stories would compete on that same voice dimension.

### Gaps
- I found no data, such as surveys or growth figures, that isolates design quality as a cause of adoption.

## Q4. Which tools offer teacher or classroom features?

### Takeaway
Classroom support is thin. Skritter is the only mainstream tool with a documented teacher dashboard: classes, assignments and progress tracking. Printable stroke-order worksheets come mostly from hobbyist, community-built generators.

### Cited Findings
- Skritter: teachers can create classes, invite students, assign lists, and view each student's progress per assignment. This comes from a secondary university page; I did not verify it against Skritter's own docs. — [MSU MAFLT page](https://sites.google.com/msu.edu/maflt-mattjohnson/pedagogical-skills/technology/skritter)
- Community worksheet generators:
  - write-kanji, a web tool shared on the WaniKani forum. — [WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/website-for-generating-printable-kanji-worksheets/38358)
  - A Jensen-style printable site with stroke-order toggling for N5–N1; its large font files load slowly. — [WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/%E2%98%85-print-kanji-practice-writing-worksheets-toggle-stroke-orders-onoff-%E2%98%85/6111)
  - kanji.sh, open source, generates sheets by JLPT, grade, WaniKani level or frequency. — [GitHub](https://github.com/rionlabs/kanji.sh)
  - An Android Kanji Worksheet Generator that outputs A4 PDFs. — [apppage](https://apppage.net/preview/com.nan.tomoe.kanjiworksheet)
  - OpenQuiz, which builds free worksheets from custom lists. — [OpenQuiz](https://openquiz.ai/learning-guides/free-kanji-writing-practice-printable.html)
- Kanji Damage's crude, NSFW tone makes it unsuitable for classroom use. — [Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-learning-resources-database/kanji-damage/)
- Kanji alive has an educational heritage: it won an Education World site award in 2005 and was used in Japanese courses. — [Education World](https://educationworld.com/awards/2005/r0405-22.shtml); [Smith College libguide](https://libguides.smith.edu/jpn221/studytools)

### Inferences
- No tool found combines classroom-safe mnemonics, projector-friendly large kanji pages, printable stroke-order sheets and shareable class sets in one free web product. That is a concrete opening for Kanji Kaisen.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether Renshuu, WaniKani or Bunpro offer teacher or class features; none were found.
- No pricing for Skritter's school plan was found.
- No tool with a "projector mode" was found.

## Q5. What gaps remain unserved?

### Takeaway
The clearest open position is a free, web-first, beautifully designed kanji reference-and-learn atlas. It would pair WaniKani-style radical stories with Skritter or Ringotan-style stroke order, without review-debt pressure, and be classroom-safe with printable and projectable outputs.

### Cited Findings (supporting evidence)
- Mnemonics and stroke order are split across tools. WaniKani has radical mnemonics but no handwriting; Ringotan and Kanji Dojo do writing but users find their hints weak. — [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/); [WaniKani community (Ringotan)](https://community.wanikani.com/t/ringotan-free-app-for-learning-how-to-write-kanji/51702)
- Free web references are dated or limited. Kanji Koohii has been maintained by one developer since 2006. Kanji alive covers only 1,235 kanji and is "not designed to be used as a dictionary". Jisho is a dictionary with no learning layer. — [Koohii](https://kanji.koohii.com/); [Kanji alive GitHub](https://github.com/kanjialive/kanji-data-media/blob/master/README.md); [90 Day Japanese](https://90dayjapanese.com/best-japanese-learning-apps/)
- Paid structure and review burnout push users toward workaround apps. — [Gakugame](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6755092872); [JLPT Lord](https://jlptlord.com/blog/wanikani-review)
- Teacher tooling is fragmented across hobbyist worksheet generators. — [kanji.sh](https://github.com/rionlabs/kanji.sh); [WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/website-for-generating-printable-kanji-worksheets/38358)

### Inferences (candidate gaps for Kanji Kaisen)
1. **Free and beautiful.** Polished competitors are paid (WaniKani, Skritter); free ones are described as plain or busy (Anki, Renshuu).
2. **Story plus stroke in one page.** No tool found pairs radical-based stories with animated stroke order and a writing canvas on the same kanji page.
3. **An atlas, not a queue.** Browsing and exploring with optional, capped review addresses review-pile burnout and gating. A low-pressure entry point would also catch learners who drop out of WaniKani.
4. **Strong mobile web.** Most writing tools are native-only (Kanji Study is Android-only; Ringotan, Kanaji and Kanji Dojo are apps), so a responsive web atlas reaches iOS, Android and desktop with one link, which also suits SEO and sharing.
5. **Classroom-safe teacher kit.** Unlike Kanji Damage's tone, it could offer printable stroke-order worksheets, shareable class sets, and a large-glyph projector view. Only Skritter, which is paid, has a teacher dashboard.
6. **Complementing, not replacing.** Users already stack tools (WaniKani + Ringotan + Bunpro), so WaniKani-level, JLPT and grade filters plus deep links let Kanji Kaisen become the go-to "look it up and understand it" page within existing stacks.

### Gaps
- No user survey quantifies demand for teacher features or for a browse-first atlas; these are inferred from complaints.
- Licensing for stroke-order data was not researched here. KanjiVG and Kanji alive's open data are candidates, but their license terms were not checked in this pass.
