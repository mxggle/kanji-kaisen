# Chinese-speaking learners of Japanese and kanji: pain points, tools, segments, and fit for Kanji Kaisen

Research date: 2026-10-31. Method: web search plus fetches. Zhihu (403), Dcard (403), Sohu (dead link), Xiaohongshu and Bilibili (not indexed by the search tool) could not be read directly. Most qualitative evidence below comes from search-engine snippets of Zhihu/PTT/Dcard pages, Taiwanese and mainland tutoring-company blogs, and app-store aggregators. The quantitative data comes from Japan Foundation (JF) primary PDFs, which were downloaded and read in full. Treat social-media claims as indicative rather than measured.

## Q1. Typical kanji difficulties for Chinese speakers (where the advantage helps and where it hurts)

### Takeaway
The Chinese advantage is real for meaning recognition. Chinese-language sources consistently name four pain points, roughly in this order: (1) readings (on/kun choice, several readings per character, 熟字訓 like 今日=きょう, voicing errors from Chinese phonology); (2) 同形異義語 false friends; (3) small glyph differences in 新字体 that cost marks on exams (歩/步, 涼/凉, 最後 written as 最后); (4) the "I can read it, so I don't need to learn it" trap, where meaning comprehension runs ahead of pronunciation. 送り仮名 is rarely raised as a separate complaint in what I could find.

### Cited Findings
**Readings (音読み/訓読み, multiple readings, 熟字訓)**
- Zhihu/Sohu explainers stress that most kanji have 2-3 readings (訓読み, 呉音, 漢音) and that 生 has 100+ readings. The "if it sounds like Chinese, it's 音読み" heuristic is unreliable: 千 read ち is 訓, read せん is 音. — [Sohu: 什么是音读和训读](https://www.sohu.com/a/287034330_778063); [Zhihu: 日语的音读和训读具体是指什么](https://www.zhihu.com/question/32189253); [Zhihu: 日语中的汉字到底该怎么读](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/484279275)
- A 2026 Taiwan-targeted guide lists "understanding the meaning but not the pronunciation" as trap #2: 今日 looks like こんにち but is read きょう; 歴史 is often mispronounced れきじ (Chinese phonology interfering with voicing); 森林=しんりん but 森 alone = もり. Trap #3 is one character with many readings (生: いきる/いっしょう/なまもの/せいぶつ/たんじょう). — [NativeCamp TW: 台灣人專屬的3大陷阱 (2026-05)](https://ja.nativecamp.net/zh-tw/blog/20260507_kanji-tips-for-taiwanese/)
- Another 2026 NativeCamp TW post says Chinese pronunciation habits unconsciously cause 清濁 confusion, and that new-style and 和製 characters are "看得懂卻不會寫" (readable but not writable). — [NativeCamp TW: 漢字怎麼背？台灣人必看的6大學習法 (2025-12)](https://ja.nativecamp.net/zh-tw/blog/20251210_kanji_learning/); [NativeCamp TW: 日文漢字3大陷阱+30組同形不同義 (2026-09)](https://ja.nativecamp.net/zh-tw/blog/20260908_japanese_kanji_traps/)
- Chinese learners circulate heuristics that map Mandarin to on'yomi, for example: a Mandarin front-nasal final (-n) tends to give ん in Japanese, and a back-nasal (-ng) tends to give a long vowel. There is a whole Zhihu series on "using Chinese pronunciation to infer 音読み". This shows demand for Chinese-specific reading bridges. — [Zhihu: 日文汉字的读音规则一览表](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/265802535); [Zhihu: 如何利用汉语读音推断日语汉字音读](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/106195770)
- On PTT C_Chat, users complain that name kanji are chaotic (one forum claim: タケル has 118 kanji spellings) and discuss why a single character can have a dozen readings. — [PTT C_Chat: 我說日文漢字寫法也太多了吧](https://www.pttweb.cc/bbs/C_Chat/M.1593315101.A.3A3) (2020, older than the target window)

**False friends (同形異義語 / 和製漢語)**
- Common examples in Chinese-language material: 爱人, 大丈夫, 得意, 外人, 経理, 情報, 手紙 (=letter). Learners "直接套用中文习惯" (apply Chinese habits directly) and misuse them. — [Zhihu: 中日同形异义词介绍 (2023)](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/642685888); [参考网: 浅析中日同形异义词](https://m.fx361.com/news/2020/0819/6966555.html); [CSDN: 日语学习概览](https://blog.csdn.net/userDengDeng/article/details/116263865)
- Taiwan-specific examples: 先生 (Taiwan = "Mr."; Japan = teacher/doctor), 丈夫 (husband vs. durable), 勉強 (Taiwanese usage can mean "discount"), plus grammatical-collocation differences (ご参考にしてください). — [NativeCamp TW (2026-05)](https://ja.nativecamp.net/zh-tw/blog/20260507_kanji-tips-for-taiwanese/)
- Education blogs warn that the advantage is partial: shared words (学生, 学校) help, but readings and usage still need real study. — [新东方前途出国: 中国人学日语真的更容易吗](https://liuxue.xdf.cn/blog/xiapei1/blog/5638195.shtml); [江户日语: 中国人学日语有优势？千万别不信](https://www.jpedo.com/news/474.html)

**Glyph differences (新字体 vs 简体/繁体) and writing correctness**
- Zhihu and Taiwanese cram-school articles titled "中国人/台灣人容易忽略的日文汉字写法" list small-stroke differences (練, 捨, 歳, 涼/凉, 強/强, 歩/步) and radical differences (語/语, 飯/饭, 紙/纸, 鉄/铁). They also list larger form changes (仏/佛, 仮/假, 販売, 芸/艺, 発 vs 髪 for 发). — [Zhihu: 中日汉字大不同，中国人容易忽略的日文汉字写法](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/55411318); [四季日本語 (TW): 台灣人容易忽略的日文漢字寫法](https://www.japanese-language.com.tw/info-136.html); [新东方: 日文汉字与中文汉字书写的不同](https://liuxue.xdf.cn/blog/chenjieqiong/blog/5009918.shtml); [Zhihu: 最易写错的十个日文汉字](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/386073065)
- Exam stakes: 高考日语 essay guidance lists "日文汉字书写错误" as a typical error (for example writing 最後 as 最后). 考研日语 scoring advice says to write kanji rather than kana where they exist, and to watch for forms that differ from Chinese to avoid losing marks. No official deduction values were found. — [网易: 高考日语作文是怎么评分的](https://m.163.com/dy/article/GL2A206705526I31.html); [新航道: 考研日语评分细则](https://cq.xhd.cn/m/info/xyz/889109.html)
- 気/气 and 図/图 did not appear in the sources I retrieved. They follow the same pattern but I have no direct citation for them.

**"Laziness" / over-reliance on the Chinese advantage**
- Tutoring-company articles frame the advantage as a double-edged sword: Chinese speakers grasp the meaning of written Japanese quickly, but this masks weak reading and listening. The NativeCamp TW framing "看得懂卻不會寫/不會唸" (can read it but can't write or say it) is typical. — [NativeCamp TW (2025-12)](https://ja.nativecamp.net/zh-tw/blog/20251210_kanji_learning/); [Zhihu: 论中国人学习日语的优势和劣势](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/477795646) (403 on fetch; title only)

### Inferences
- A tool's highest-value features for this audience are readings-first: which reading applies in which word, 熟字訓 flags, voicing (連濁/清濁) traps, and Mandarin-to-on'yomi correspondence hints. Meaning glosses matter far less. "Radical stories" mostly serve English learners, since Chinese speakers already know the characters.
- A "差异字" (difference) layer would directly target a known exam pain point: a glyph-diff view of 日本新字体 vs 简体 vs 繁体 with highlighted strokes, plus a 同形異義 warning badge on example words.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifies how many Chinese learners rank readings versus false friends versus glyphs as their top problem. All evidence is qualitative.
- No 2022-2026 Xiaohongshu or Bilibili posts could be read directly. Laziness and over-reliance are described mostly by tutoring companies, which have a commercial interest in saying that the advantage is a trap.
- 送り仮名 errors as a Chinese-specific complaint: no sources found.

## Q2. Popular apps/resources and their complaints

### Takeaway
MOJi辞書 is the dominant Chinese-native dictionary/flashcard app (about 113k ratings on the China App Store, 4.8 stars). Complaints focus on paywalls, tiered memberships, dictionary-quality accusations (older), and slow support. 沪江 has declined: 小D词典 appears to be gone and 开心词场 is stale. Anki is used heavily by more serious learners via Chinese community-made decks. Kanji-specific tools in Chinese are thin, mostly small stroke-order/dictionary apps and Japanese school-kanji sites.

### Cited Findings
- MOJi辞書: China App Store about 4.8 stars on 113,376 ratings (aggregator data); Google Play about 3.97 on 2,811 ratings. — [chartoo CN](https://cn.chartoo.com/itunes/app/1021094295-moji%E8%BE%9E%E6%9B%B8-%E6%97%A5%E8%AF%AD%E5%AD%A6%E4%B9%A0%E8%AF%8D%E5%85%B8); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mojitec.mojidict&hl=en_US)
- An older 少数派 review praised MOJi's UI, shared word lists and daily push. It noted that because Japanese is a "小语种" (minor language), the choice of vocabulary apps is narrow compared with English. — [少数派: MOJi辞书 App+1](https://sspai.com/post/45299) (2018, outside the window)
- Zhihu complaints about MOJi membership (an answer about six years old): accusations of copied or error-prone entries and inaccurate audio; social-ified favorites; PDF export limited to loaded words; a new higher membership tier; an "extended dictionary" popping up behind a further paywall. The developer replied in-thread. — [Zhihu: Moji辞書和MojiTest会员版体验如何](https://www.zhihu.com/question/429559211/answer/3093549704)
- Dcard (Taiwan) user complaint: bug reports met only with canned "感謝親，已經收到" replies for weeks, and support requires binding WeChat Work (企业微信). The mainland-Chinese tone and support channel grate on Taiwanese users. — Dcard post [259419248](https://www.dcard.tw/f/japanese/p/259419248) (403 on fetch; content from the search snippet; date not verified)
- Many third-party sites distribute "MOJi 会员免费版 / 永久会员" cracked APKs, which signals strong demand plus paywall resistance. — e.g. [xinhuaedu](https://www.xinhuaedu.cn/android/303mojicsyfdd.html), [lhdown](https://www.lhdown.com/shouji/118780.html)
- 沪江: 开心词场 removed its K-12 English books in Jan 2022 citing policy changes. Japanese content was still present in v6.17.19 (2024-04). A Zhihu answer and a 52pojie thread say 沪江小D词典 no longer works and was reportedly delisted (unverified). Zhihu users call 开心词场's review function weaker than other apps. — [App Store TW listing](https://apps.apple.com/tw/app/%E6%B2%AA%E6%B1%9F%E5%BC%80%E5%BF%83%E8%AF%8D%E5%9C%BA-%E5%A4%9A%E8%AF%AD%E7%A7%8D%E8%B6%A3%E5%91%B3%E9%97%AF%E5%85%B3%E8%83%8C%E5%8D%95%E8%AF%8D/id635206028); [downkuai v6.17.19](https://www.downkuai.com/android/82912.html); [Zhihu: 沪江小d词典?](https://www.zhihu.com/question/665236654/answer/69172600590); [52pojie](https://www.52pojie.cn/thread-1978828-1-1.html); [Zhihu: 用沪江开心词场背日语到底有用吗](https://www.zhihu.com/question/346377134)
- Anki: Chinese communities share JLPT N5-N1 decks built on 新世纪日汉 and 大辞泉 dictionaries with furigana, audio and context sentences (FreeMdict, LINUX DO, Zhihu, Dcard). Kanji-only decks recommended for Chinese learners tend to be English-language ones (Heisig RTK, KKLC). — [FreeMdict: 日语语境智能牌组](https://forum.freemdict.com/t/topic/29593); [LINUX DO: GitHub 日语 Anki 卡组 N5-N1](https://linux.do/t/topic/773211); [Zhihu: Anki2日语卡片分享](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/682555712); [Dcard: Anki 使用大全](https://www.dcard.tw/f/japanese/p/257694449); [japantruly: best Japanese Anki decks](https://shop.japantruly.com/zh/blogs/product-review/best-japanese-anki-decks)
- Bilibili: Zhihu threads recommending B站 Japanese courses center on 五十音 and 新版标准日本语 (新标日) series. Third-party sites repackage a 195-episode "B站零基础入门日语" collection. — [Zhihu: B站上有哪些好的日语教学视频](https://www.zhihu.com/question/59927188/answers/updated); [Zhihu: B站系统一点的up主推荐](https://www.zhihu.com/question/355445298); [tgoos](https://www.tgoos.com/45236)
- Traditional-Chinese kanji-specific apps that Taiwanese blogs recommend: 日本漢字辭典 (iOS; about 6,700 characters, animated stroke order, search by radical/stroke/kana), Kanji123, 學習日語漢字 (POROstudio; N5-N1 with stroke order, on/kun, example words, quiz), Obenkyo, Kanji Dojo. — [App Store: 日本漢字辭典](https://apps.apple.com/tw/app/%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E6%BC%A2%E5%AD%97%E8%BE%AD%E5%85%B8/id6746325244); [briian: Kanji123](https://briian.com/48511/); [briian: 學習日語漢字](https://briian.com/78270/); [NativeCamp TW: 10款免費日語APP](https://ja.nativecamp.net/zh-tw/blog/20251216_japanese_learning_apps/)
- Web resources recommended in Chinese "收藏级日语网站" lists for kanji: yomikatawa (reading lookup), 漢字ドリルのJAKKA (1,026 教育漢字 with stroke charts and printable PDFs), OJAD, Weblio, MOJi web. — [少数派: 学日语的免费网站](https://sspai.com/post/58038); [maigoo 2026 ranking](https://www.maigoo.com/top/435790.html); [Zhihu: 18个日语学习网站](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/151024714)

### Inferences
- POROstudio's 學習日語漢字 already offers N5-N1 stroke order + on/kun + example words + quiz in Traditional Chinese. On features, Kanji Kaisen would not be novel to Taiwanese users. Its differentiation would have to be design quality, free/no-paywall positioning (the MOJi paywall resentment is a usable opening), and Chinese-specific content (difference highlighting, Mandarin-to-on'yomi hints).
- No Chinese-native web "kanji atlas" with an aesthetic focus surfaced in searches. That is a possible gap, but search coverage of Xiaohongshu was nil, so absence is weak evidence.

### Gaps
- Could not read app-store review text for MOJi, 新标日 apps, or 五十音 apps in 2024-2026. Complaint themes rely on older Zhihu and Dcard posts.
- No usage-share data comparing MOJi, Anki, 沪江 and others.

## Q3. Segment sizes, motivations, and student vs working-adult share

### Takeaway
Mainland China is the world's largest institutional market (1,019,197 learners in 2024, down 3.6%). School-based learning is shrinking about 15% while non-school learning (private schools, emigration prep) is growing. Self-learners via apps and videos are "相当数" (a considerable number) but uncounted. Taiwan has 124,149 institutional learners (down 13.6%) and Hong Kong saw a 24.6% drop. Motivations cited by JF: study abroad, Japanese-company jobs, emigration to Japan, ACG/pop culture, tourism, and in mainland China a strategic gaokao subject choice. No reliable student vs working-adult split exists.

### Cited Findings
- JF 2024 survey (conducted Sep-Dec 2024, released 2025-09-04): world total 4,000,750 learners. China #1 with 1,019,197 (−38,121, −3.6%). Taiwan #8 with 124,149 (−19,483, −13.6%). Hong Kong −6,797 (−24.6%), 4th-largest decline worldwide. — [JF press release 2025-031](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/about/press/2025/dl/2025-031.pdf)
- China detail: secondary −51,268 and higher education −79,724 learners (each about 15% down). Non-school institutions +94,164 learners (+58.5%) and +35.1% institutions. (The arithmetic, −51k −80k +94k ≈ −38k, shows 94,164 is the increase, not the total.) Reasons given: Japanese firms withdrawing and rising Chinese wages reduce the payoff; generative AI driving closures of language departments; the gaokao Japanese exam becoming harder, so it is no longer the "easy-score" alternative to English. Non-school growth is attributed to rising emigration to Japan and post-2021 recovery of tutoring. — [JF 中国 (2025年度) country page](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/survey/area/country/2025/china_2.pdf)
- China: JF says the institutional count excludes 独学者 (self-learners), who "かなりの数に達する" (reach considerable numbers). Self-study textbooks, free apps and explanation videos are widely available, and app/video self-learners are "相当数" but uncounted. Adults also study for promotion and certifications. — [JF 中国 (2025年度)](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/survey/area/country/2025/china_2.pdf)
- China motivations per JF: study in Japan, jobs at Japanese firms, plus anime/manga, fashion, pop culture and tourism. China's distinguishing feature is that higher education holds the largest share of learners and many reach intermediate/advanced level (second-foreign-language students reach about N4; first-foreign-language about N2; majors about N1 by graduation). — [JF 中国 (2025年度)](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/survey/area/country/2025/china_2.pdf)
- China JLPT: about 300,000 examinees in FY2025. — [JF 中国 (2025年度)](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/survey/area/country/2025/china_2.pdf)
- Taiwan 2024 breakdown: primary 3,018; secondary 31,669; higher 52,161; non-school 37,301; total 124,149. Japanese is the most-studied foreign language after English. Among senior-high second foreign languages (2023), 93.2% of schools offering one include Japanese, and Japanese has 48.2% of learners (24,634 of 51,118). JLPT 2024: 76,149 applicants and 66,514 examinees (slightly down), spread fairly evenly across N1-N5. About 6.76M Taiwanese visited Japan in 2025. Declines are blamed on the falling birthrate, English-strengthening policy, and a STEM shift. — [JF 台湾 (2025年度)](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/survey/area/country/2025/taiwan_3.pdf); [JF press release](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/about/press/2025/dl/2025-031.pdf)
- Older JF-derived figure (2018, overseas higher-ed learners overall, not China-specific): 76.7% cite interest in Japanese pop culture (anime/manga/music/fashion) and about 69% cite study in Japan. — [Melbourne Asia Review](https://www.melbourneasiareview.edu.au/%E6%BE%B3%E5%A4%A7%E5%88%A9%E4%BA%9A%E5%8F%8A%E5%85%B6%E4%BB%96%E6%B5%B7%E5%A4%96%E6%97%A5%E8%AF%AD%E5%AD%A6%E4%B9%A0%E8%80%85%E7%9A%84%E5%8A%A8%E5%8A%9B%E4%BB%8E%E4%BD%95%E8%80%8C%E6%9D%A5/) (secondary citation; not verified against the JF original)
- Chinese questionnaires (问卷星/问卷网) consistently offer the same motivation options: ACG/pop culture interest, study abroad, 考研/further study, employment, 高考调剂 (being reassigned to a Japanese major after the gaokao), going to Japan for work. This confirms the segment taxonomy but provides no results. — [wjx: 日语专业学生学习动机与认知变化问卷](https://www.wjx.cn/vm/OJKnilZ.aspx); [wjx: 日语专业学生就业现状问卷](https://www.wjx.cn/vm/wFtqCgK.aspx)

### Inferences
- Hong Kong's 2021 base can be derived as about 27,600 (6,797 / 0.246), which puts 2024 at about 20,800 institutional learners (my calculation, not a published figure).
- The growing mainland segment is out-of-school adults: self-learners, emigration/work-in-Japan preppers, and JLPT takers. These people are reachable via apps and social media rather than institutions, which suits a free web tool. The shrinking segment is gaokao and university students.
- Mainland JLPT volume (about 300k per year) is roughly 4-5 times Taiwan's (about 66k). Mainland China is the larger addressable market, but it needs Simplified Chinese and China-accessible hosting.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2022-2026 data on the share of students vs working adults among Chinese learners, or on the share of ACG-motivated learners. JF counts only institutions.
- Overseas Chinese (Malaysia/Singapore/North America heritage speakers): no specific data found.
- The total Taiwan figure in the press release and the country page match (124,149), but JF notes that country-page figures may differ from the report.

## Q4. What goes viral on Xiaohongshu/Bilibili/Douyin for 日语学习, and is there demand for an aesthetic kanji tool?

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. Xiaohongshu and Bilibili content was not reachable through the search tooling. Indirect evidence shows that Xiaohongshu language-learning content is female-skewed and keyword-driven, that "small-language study tips" is a recognized high-performing topic category, and that 五十音 and 新标日 are the most-collected Bilibili Japanese content. I found no direct evidence of viral 汉字词源 (character-etymology) or aesthetic-kanji content for Japanese learners.

### Cited Findings
- A Xiaohongshu topic list includes "日语韩语西班牙语等小众语言学习技巧" (Japanese/Korean/Spanish and other niche-language learning tips) among 30 common viral 学习笔记 (study-note) topics. — [爱运营: 小红书常见的30种爆款选题](https://www.iyunying.org/operate/xiaohongshu/342118.html)
- A Zhihu case study of Xiaohongshu language-education accounts uses 蔡蔡日语老师 as a benchmark. It notes keyword-stuffed titles ("日语", "学习") for search exposure and a high female-user share matching beginner Japanese content (personal-experience post; data unverified). — [Zhihu: 关于在小红书如何快速变现](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/1419840884)
- Xiaohongshu virality playbooks stress visual contrast and comparison ("对比效果") and sensory/visual appeal. This lines up with side-by-side "中日汉字写法对比" (Chinese vs Japanese form comparison) style content, though that is my inference. — [人人都是产品经理: 小红书爆款笔记借鉴灵感](https://www.woshipm.com/operate/5982581.html)
- "日语学习资料" (selling or sharing Japanese study materials) is marketed as a side-hustle niche, and an indie developer reportedly earned over 10k RMB from an AI Japanese-learning website. This is low-quality and promotional, but suggests a monetizable audience exists. — [dklx 副业](https://www.dklx.com/21131.html); [redcao](https://www.redcao.com/archives/35830.html)
- Bilibili: the most-collected Japanese teaching videos on Zhihu recommendation lists are 五十音 and 标准日本语 courses. — [Zhihu: B站上有哪些好的日语教学视频](https://www.zhihu.com/question/59927188/answers/updated)
- 手帐 culture originated with the Japanese 手帳 and is popular on both Bilibili and Xiaohongshu, a possible cross-over hook. — [人物宝典: 手帐火遍B站小红书](https://ren.yxbaodian.com/view/shou_zhang.html)

### Inferences
- Shareable visual formats likely to fit Xiaohongshu are side-by-side 新字体/简体/繁体 diff cards, "同形异义词" warning cards, and "这个字日语怎么读" (how is this read in Japanese) reading cards. An aesthetically strong kanji atlas that exports cards or images could seed such posts. This is a hypothesis, not demonstrated demand.
- Radical-story mnemonics, Kanji Kaisen's core English-audience hook, are probably less compelling for Chinese speakers, who already know the characters. Etymology content may still appeal as cultural interest, but I found no evidence of virality.

### Gaps
- No view, like, or save counts for any 汉字/日语 post on Xiaohongshu, Bilibili, or Douyin. Direct platform access, or a scraping tool such as 新红/千瓜 data, would be needed.
- No evidence either way on demand for an "aesthetically beautiful" kanji tool among Chinese speakers.

## Q5. Is a Chinese UI necessary to reach this audience?

### Takeaway
Almost certainly yes for mainland China and very likely for Taiwan and Hong Kong. Every popular tool found (MOJi, 沪江, Taiwanese kanji apps, Chinese Anki decks built on 新世纪日汉) is Chinese-native. English-language kanji resources circulate only among a subset of serious learners. The work is more than translating the UI: definitions and glosses need Chinese, with Simplified and Traditional variants and region-appropriate tone. Mainland reach also depends on network accessibility.

### Cited Findings
- Chinese learners' Anki decks are built on Chinese-Japanese dictionaries (新世纪日汉双解) with Chinese definitions. Kanji-specific decks that get recommended are English (RTK/KKLC), which shows a gap in Chinese kanji-specific materials. — [FreeMdict](https://forum.freemdict.com/t/topic/29593); [japantruly](https://shop.japantruly.com/zh/blogs/product-review/best-japanese-anki-decks)
- Taiwanese kanji apps recommended by local blogs are all Traditional-Chinese UI (日本漢字辭典, 學習日語漢字, Kanji123). — [App Store TW](https://apps.apple.com/tw/app/%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E6%BC%A2%E5%AD%97%E8%BE%AD%E5%85%B8/id6746325244); [briian](https://briian.com/78270/)
- Taiwanese users object to mainland-style service conventions (canned "親" replies, WeChat Work support) in MOJi, so regional localization matters. — [Dcard snippet](https://www.dcard.tw/f/japanese/p/259419248)
- JF notes that many Chinese self-learners rely on free apps and explanation videos available domestically. — [JF 中国 (2025年度)](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/survey/area/country/2025/china_2.pdf)
- Chinese learners specifically want reading bridges from Mandarin (front/back nasal → ん/long vowel). Only Chinese-language content can deliver this. — [Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/265802535)

### Inferences
- An English-UI Kanji Kaisen would mainly reach English-proficient overseas Chinese and a minority of advanced mainland/Taiwan learners. A zh-Hans + zh-Hant UI with Chinese glosses, a "中日字形差异" panel, and Chinese-specific reading tips would be the minimum to compete. Kanji Kaisen's existing per-kanji data (on/kun, stroke order, example words) carries over directly; glosses and radical stories would need rewriting rather than literal translation.
- Mainland distribution realistically runs through Xiaohongshu and Bilibili posts linking to a China-accessible domain. Whether the current hosting (likely Vercel) is reliably reachable from the mainland is untested and is a practical blocker to check.

### Gaps
- No data on the English proficiency of Chinese Japanese learners, or on how many use English-UI tools such as Jisho, WaniKani, or Renshuu.
- No data on whether Chinese learners would accept a web tool versus an app or WeChat mini-program, though mini-programs are a common distribution channel in China.
