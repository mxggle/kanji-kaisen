図鑑Round 6 · the system every page uses

One atlas.
One hand.

Every page is a plate in the same book. It has the same path at the top, the same specimen beside the same reading, and the same three objects: a theme gate, a radical seal and a kanji tile. Five rules decide every colour, line, fill and glow, so a learner who understands one page can read all of them.

五則

Five rules

If a design choice isn't covered here, it should be ink and line, not colour.

1 · Colour = theme

A page takes the colour of the theme it belongs to. Nothing else gets a colour: buttons are ink, JLPT levels are plain tags. In 墨 every theme becomes one vermilion.

2 · Line = role

氵watermeaning 毎マイ→カイsound

A solid line marks the part that carries meaning, and a dashed line marks the part that carries sound. This is the method, drawn the same way on every page.

3 · Fill = your ink

白 淡 中 濃 印

How well you know a kanji is shown by how dark it is, and that's the only progress mark. Groups show one painted bar. There are no rings, percentages or streaks.

4 · Italic = story

A man leaning against a tree.Origin · Wiktionary

“Rest in the shade of a tree.”Memory story

Sourced etymology is set in roman type with its source. Mnemonics are always italic and labelled, so readers never mistake a story for a fact.

5 · Glow = live

stroke 2 of 4,
being drawn

Only something that is moving glows: a stroke being drawn, or the step you're on. Headings, buttons and cards never glow. In 墨 nothing glows.
